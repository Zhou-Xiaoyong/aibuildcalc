How to Use This Calculator

Flooring costs depend on material type, grade, and whether you include professional installation. This calculator uses 2026 national cost data to estimate your total flooring project cost.

  1. Enter the total floor area in square feet
  2. Select the flooring material type
  3. Choose the grade level (budget, mid-range, or premium)
  4. Indicate whether to include professional installation
  5. Select your regional cost factor
Expert Insights Smart analysis for your flooring project
🏆
Hardwood = Best ROI
Hardwood floors return 70-80% of cost at resale — the highest of any flooring. Buyers strongly prefer hardwood in living areas and bedrooms.
💰
LVP = Best Budget Pick
Luxury vinyl plank offers the look of hardwood at 40-60% less cost. It's waterproof, scratch-resistant, and perfect for kitchens, bathrooms, and basements.
📐
Waste Factor Matters
Always buy 10% extra for cuts and waste. For complex patterns (diagonal, herringbone), buy 15% extra. Running out mid-project causes shade mismatches.

Calculation Methodology

Material Cost = Area x Material Price per Sq Ft (by type) Grade Multiplier: Good x0.85, Better x1.0, Best x1.3 Installation = Material x 0.55 (labor ~35% of total installed) Total = (Material + Installation) x Location Factor If DIY: Total = Material x Grade x Location Factor

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to install flooring?
The national average flooring installation cost is $3-$22 per square foot, including materials and labor. Laminate and vinyl plank cost $5-$12 per sq ft, carpet $4-$12, engineered hardwood $10-$19, solid hardwood $13-$23, ceramic tile $8-$18, and natural stone $16-$35 per sq ft.
What is the cheapest flooring?
Laminate and luxury vinyl plank (LVP) are the cheapest flooring options at $5-$12 per square foot installed. Carpet is also affordable at $4-$12 per sq ft (including pad). Ceramic tile costs $8-$18, engineered hardwood $10-$19, solid hardwood $13-$23, and natural stone $16-$35 per sq ft.
Which flooring has the best ROI?
Hardwood flooring has the highest ROI, returning 70-80% of cost at resale. Buyers prefer hardwood in living areas and bedrooms. Tile is preferred in kitchens and bathrooms and returns 60-70%. Carpet has the lowest ROI at 50-60%.

How to Use This Calculator

Fence costs depend on material type, height, length, and number of gates. This calculator uses 2026 national cost data to estimate your total fence installation cost.

  1. Measure the total linear footage of fencing needed
  2. Select the fence material type
  3. Choose the fence height
  4. Select the number and type of gates
  5. Choose your regional cost factor
Expert Insights Smart analysis for your fence project
🏡
Vinyl = Low Maintenance
Vinyl fences cost ~50% more than wood upfront but never need staining or painting. Over 20 years, vinyl often costs less than wood when factoring in maintenance.
📏
Height & Privacy Rules
Most cities limit front-yard fences to 4 feet and back/side yards to 6 feet. 8-foot fences usually require a variance. Check local codes before installing.
🚪
Gate Costs Add Up
A single walk gate adds $200-$400, and a double drive gate $800-$1,500. Plan gate locations carefully — extra gates significantly increase total project cost.

Calculation Methodology

Base Cost = Length x Cost per Linear Foot (by type) Height Factor: 4ft x0.8, 6ft x1.0, 8ft x1.3 Gate Costs: Walk gate $300, Drive gate $1,150 Fence Cost = Base x Height Factor x Location Factor Materials = Fence Cost x 0.60, Labor = Fence Cost x 0.40 Total = Fence Cost + Gates

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to install a fence?
The national average fence installation cost is $1,500-$4,000 for 150 linear feet. Wood privacy fences cost $15-$45 per linear foot, vinyl $30-$60, chain link $15-$30, aluminum $40-$70, and wrought iron $50-$100 per linear foot installed.
What is the cheapest fence to install?
Chain link is the cheapest fence at $15-$30 per linear foot installed. Wood picket fences are also affordable at $15-$30 per foot. Wood privacy fences cost $25-$45 per foot. Vinyl and aluminum are premium options at $30-$70 per foot.
How long does a fence last?
Chain link: 15-20 years. Wood: 10-15 years (with staining). Vinyl: 20-30 years. Aluminum: 20-30 years. Wrought iron: 50+ years with proper maintenance. Vinyl and aluminum offer the best balance of durability and low maintenance.

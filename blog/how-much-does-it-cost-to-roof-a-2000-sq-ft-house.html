How Much Does It Cost to Roof a 2000 Sq Ft House

Published August 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 2,000 square foot house is one of the most common roof-replacement projects in the US. The catch is that "how much" depends almost entirely on the material you choose and the roof's pitch, not on the floor plan. A 2,000 sq ft home with a moderate 6/12 pitch has roughly 22 to 24 squares of roof area, and at 2026 prices that means anywhere from about $9,000 for basic asphalt to about $50,000 for premium standing-seam metal or tile. This guide gives you per-square ranges by material, the extras that move the total, and a worked breakdown you can take to three local bidders.

Quick answer: In 2026 a 2,000 sq ft home with a moderate pitch costs roughly $9,000-$15,000 for asphalt shingles, $22,000-$33,000 for standing-seam metal, and $25,000-$50,000 for tile or slate. Estimate your exact squares first with the Roofing Calculator.

The first thing to sort out: roof area vs. floor area

Floor area and roof area are not the same. A 2,000 sq ft single-story home may have a roof footprint roughly equal to the floor plan, but the actual roof surface is always larger because of pitch: the steeper the roof, the more material you need. The relationship is captured by the pitch multiplier, and on a typical 6/12 pitch a 2,000 sq ft home has about 22 to 24 squares of roof (a square = 100 sq ft of roof surface).

Asphalt is the default and cheapest; metal and tile run two to four times the asphalt figure but last roughly double the lifespan, which is why many homeowners look at lifetime cost rather than the upfront number. The ranges are installed costs including tear-off, underlayment, flashing, and labor — not just material.

What makes a 2,000 sq ft roof cost more or less

Several line items move the total well beyond the basic square footage:

  • Pitch: a 12/12 pitch has roughly 1.41x the material area of a low-slope roof; cost goes up nearly proportionally.
  • Tear-off: removing one existing layer runs $1-3 per square foot of roof surface; two layers can double that.
  • Roof geometry: a simple gable is the cheapest; hips, valleys, dormers, and skylights each add labor and flashing.
  • Underlayment and code upgrades: ice and water shield at eaves, valleys, and penetrations adds $1-2 per square foot of roof.
  • Plywood/OSB decking replacement: damage found at tear-off runs $75-$110 per 4x8 sheet installed.
  • Ventilation: adding ridge vent or upgrading attic ventilation can add $300-$1,000 but extends shingle life.
  • Region: coastal and Northeast projects run 20-40% above the national average on labor; Great Plains and Southern projects run 5-15% below.

Worked example: a 2,000 sq ft home, 6/12 pitch, architectural shingles

Roof footprint = 2,000 sq ft Pitch multiplier (6/12) = 1.118 Roof area = 2,000 x 1.118 = 2,236 sq ft = 22.4 squares Material at $550/square = 22.4 x $550 = $12,320 Tear-off one layer = 22.4 x $200 = $4,480 Ice & water shield (eaves/valleys/pipes) = ~$1,000 Drip edge, flashing = ~$500 Ventilation upgrade = ~$500 Permit & disposal fee = ~$300 ESTIMATED TOTAL = ~$19,100

Round to $18,500-$22,000 for a fair bidder range in a typical US Midwest market. California, the Northeast, and Hawaii will sit 25-40% higher; the South and Plains 5-15% lower.

Asphalt vs. metal, a 25-year view

Architectural asphalt at $13,000 installed with a 30-year life costs about $430 per year to own. Standing-seam metal at $25,000 with a 50-year life costs about $500 per year — more annually if you finance, but rarely a second replacement in a lifetime. The math is even more favorable if you plan to stay in the home past the first asphalt lifespan. The Metal Roof Calculator lets you compare a metal retrofit directly to your asphalt baseline.

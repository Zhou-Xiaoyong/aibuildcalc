How to Calculate Flooring Square Footage With Waste

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 12 by 20 ft room is 240 sq ft. Order 240 sq ft of tile and the last row comes up short, because every cut along a wall, a door jamb and a register eats material that never reaches the floor. The multiplier is the whole skill, and for most rooms it is 10 percent.

Quick answer: Measure each room at its widest point, multiply length by width, add the closets, then apply a waste factor. Ten percent covers a rectangular room in a straight lay. 240 sq ft at 10 percent is 264 sq ft, or 22 cartons of 12 sq ft tile. The Tile Calculator runs the order and adds thinset and grout.

Measure the room the way the last row needs it

Pull the tape at the widest point in each direction, not along the baseboard. A room in a house built before 1980 is rarely square, and a 12 ft width can read 12 ft 3 in at one end. The wide end sets the length of every full piece in that row.

Include closets and the footprint under the range and refrigerator. Appliances sit on the finished floor, and a refrigerator alcove is often 32 sq ft that a fast sketch misses.

The waste factor, by layout

Waste is not a cushion for sloppy math. It pays for cuts, breakage and the pieces you cannot buy later.

LayoutWaste factorWhy it climbs
Straight lay, open rectangle5%Four walls, no jogs, cuts land in one row
Straight lay, standard room10%Doorway, closet, one or two jogs
Plank with random stagger8%Stagger eats 6 to 12 in at every row end
45 degree layout15%Every edge cut is a triangle
Herringbone or chevron15% - 20%Pattern starts square and has to finish clean
Any room under 50 sq ft15%Same cuts, bigger share of a small floor
Stairs, wrapped tread and riser20%Nose wrap and pattern match at each step

A diagonal layout or a room with six corners pushes the factor up one row. A 40 sq ft powder room sits at 15 percent however it is laid: the cuts are a fixed cost and the floor is small.

The arithmetic, with a full carton count

Net sq ft = (length x width) for each room, added together Order sq ft = net sq ft x (1 + waste factor) Cartons = order sq ft / carton coverage, rounded up Carpet sq yd = order sq ft / 9 Worked example, 12 ft x 20 ft room, 12x24 porcelain at 12 sq ft per carton: 12 x 20 = 240 sq ft 240 x 1.10 = 264 sq ft 264 / 12 = 22 cartons 22 x 12 = 264 sq ft on hand, 24 sq ft spare

Carton coverage is printed on the end of the box and runs 5.4 sq ft on a 6x24 plank to 24 sq ft on large-format tile. Guess the coverage instead and a 240 sq ft room ends up four cartons short on a Saturday.

The hardwood row shows where rounding lands: 13.2 cartons becomes 14, so 20 sq ft of that order is arithmetic rather than cutting, and on a $5.80 floor that is $116.

Carpet is a roll problem, not a carton problem

Carpet sells by the square yard and arrives on a 12 ft or 15 ft roll, so roll width decides the waste. A 15 ft roll covers a 12 ft room in one piece and takes 20 linear ft, or 33 sq yd, with a 3 ft strip left over. The same room in 12 ft goods needs two pieces and a seam: 40 linear ft, or 53 sq yd. That seam costs 20 sq yd, about $440 at $22 a yard, which explains a high carpet bid on a 250 sq ft room. The Carpet Calculator returns both numbers.

Where the estimate goes wrong

  • Deducting openings and fixtures. Flooring runs through a doorway to the transition strip, so a 3 ft opening is not a deduction, and a toilet still needs cuts around it.
  • Buying across two dye lots. Tile is fired in batches and laminate is printed in runs, and two lots can differ enough to show a line down the middle of a room. Keep the order on one purchase.
  • Skipping the spare carton. One unopened carton in the garage costs $60 to $130. Without it, a repair after a supply line leak means a new floor; the pattern is gone in three years.
  • Measuring the floor and not the prep. Self-leveler is priced on the same square footage, about 50 sq ft per 50 lb bag at 1/8 in, and takes the same 10 percent.

What to order

Measure net, add 10 percent, divide by the printed carton coverage, and round up. Use 15 percent for a 45 degree layout or a room with six corners. Buy the whole order at once, from one lot, and keep one carton sealed.

Two checks belong before that order. On tile, confirm the floor passes L/360 for ceramic or L/720 for stone with the Floor Joist Calculator, since no waste factor fixes a floor that flexes. Then read the return policy: most retailers take back unopened cartons inside 30 days and nothing opened, so 15 percent on a diagonal nets out near 10 percent. The trade-off is a second trip, which is cheaper than a discontinued pattern in year three. Every estimator that does this math sits on the flooring category page.

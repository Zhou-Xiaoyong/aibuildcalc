How Much Does It Cost to Refinish Hardwood Floors

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Nine hundred square feet of 1965 red oak, three coats of water-based finish, and a $2,925 bid from a two-man crew outside Columbus. The same floor bids $5,949 outside Boston. Nothing happened to the wood. The rate per square foot and the four line items inside it changed.

Quick answer: Sanding and finishing a hardwood floor runs $3.25 to $6.50 per square foot in 2026 for a three-coat water-based system, $5 to $8 with a stain, and $1.50 to $2.50 for a screen-and-recoat that never touches bare wood. Nine hundred square feet at $4.50 is $4,050. The Hardwood Floor Calculator turns room dimensions into the floor area that rate gets multiplied by.

Three jobs, three prices, one word

Refinishing covers work that shares almost nothing except a sander in the driveway, and the scopes price a factor of three apart.

A recoat is legitimate only over a finish that still seals. Drop water on the floor: if it beads, the coat is intact; if the wood darkens in 30 seconds, the sealer is gone and a recoat shows a color line within a year.

What the rate buys

Labor is 45 to 55 percent of the rate. A 900 sq ft open floor takes one long day to sand with a drum sander, an edger and a buffer, and most of that day goes to edges and corners. Finish is the small line: about 6 gallons for three coats, $300 to $500.

The arithmetic, on 900 sq ft

Floor sq ft = sum of room areas (measure to the wall, include closets) Sanding and finishing = floor sq ft x rate Stain = floor sq ft x $0.50 to $1.50 Board repair = boards x $8 to $15 each Stairs = $35 to $65 per tread Extra coat = $0.60 per sq ft Worked example, 900 sq ft of red oak, three rooms, 12 boards replaced, 14 steps: 900 x $4.50 = $4,050 sand and three coats, water-based 900 x $0.75 = $675 stain 12 x $12 = $144 board replacement 14 x $45 = $630 treads total $5,499 plus $450 for HEPA dust containment = $5,949

Those four lines are why two bids for one address can differ by $3,000 and both be honest.

What moves the number more than area

Small floors cost more per square foot. A 300 sq ft bedroom carries the same setup, trip charge and half-day of coating, so it prices near the top of the band.

Condition is the second lever. Carpet tack strip, staples and adhesive add half a day of scraping. A waxed or shellacked 1950s floor has to be stripped first, because wax loads the abrasive in minutes.

Stairs, thresholds and closet tracks price per piece: a 14-tread run at $45 is $630 for about 60 sq ft.

When sanding is not on the table

Wear layer is the hard limit. A 3/4 in solid oak plank carries about a quarter inch above the tongue, and a drum sander takes 1/32 in per pass, so four to six sandings across the life of the floor. A 2 mm engineered veneer gets one, maybe two. Under 1 mm of veneer is not a refinishing candidate.

Cupping is the second. Find the moisture source before the sander arrives: wood should read 6 to 9 percent moisture content and sit within 2 to 4 points of the subfloor. A floor that flexes underfoot is a framing question, and the Floor Joist Calculator checks span and deflection before anyone pays for a finish.

Lead paint is the third. The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule puts any job sanding more than 6 sq ft of painted surface in one room of a pre-1978 home under RRP certification. Painted baseboards and risers trip it, and a certified bid carries $400 to $900 of containment.

Pet stains sit in the top 1/16 in, and sanding them out can take the floor below its wear limit. Where the boards are past saving, replacement costs less than the repair. Price a carpeted room with the Carpet Calculator.

Bid A saves $3,024 and hands back two problems. Two coats instead of three means a recoat in five years, not eight, and the 14 treads return as a change order once the furniture is out.

What to book

If the finish beads water across the whole floor and no lane has gone grey, book the recoat: $1,350 to $2,250 on 900 sq ft, a day and a half of work, five to seven years of life. If any traffic lane has worn to bare wood, book the full sand with three coats, because a recoat over a bare lane fails visibly and you pay for both jobs.

The trade-off is time: three to four days of an empty house and 24 hours before furniture goes back. Water-based finish costs 10 to 15 percent more than oil-modified and wants its recoat sooner. On a floor you plan to keep, pay for the third coat now. Every other estimator for the job sits on the flooring category page.

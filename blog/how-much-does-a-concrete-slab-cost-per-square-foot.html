How Much Does a Concrete Slab Cost Per Square Foot

Published July 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A concrete slab is one of the most common home improvement investments, but “cost per square foot” varies widely by thickness, finish, and whether you pour ready-mix or mix bags by hand. This guide breaks down realistic 2025–2026 US price ranges so you can budget with confidence.

Quick answer: A 4-inch residential slab typically runs $6–$10 per square foot installed, while a 6-inch slab runs $8–$13 per square foot. Use our Concrete Slab Calculator to estimate your exact project cost.

The short answer by thickness

Most patios, walkways, and shed floors use a 4-inch slab. Driveways and anything that sees vehicle traffic usually call for 5–6 inches. Because cost scales almost directly with the amount of concrete, a thicker slab costs proportionally more in both material and labor. A plain, broom-finished gray slab is the budget option; a stained, stamped, or exposed-aggregate finish can add $2–$8 per square foot on top of the base price.

For a 10x10 foot pad (100 sq ft), that works out to roughly $600–$1,000 for a 4-inch slab and $800–$1,300 for a 6-inch slab, installed. Your regional labor rate moves the total far more than the concrete price does, so treat these as planning ranges rather than firm quotes.

Ready-mix vs bagged concrete

How you buy the concrete changes the math. A single 80-pound bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet, so one cubic yard (27 cubic feet) needs roughly 45 bags. At $4–$6 per bag, the material alone for a cubic yard runs $180–$270. Ready-mix delivered is typically $110–$165 per cubic yard, and the plant controls the mix quality.

What else affects the price

  • Site prep: Grading, excavation, and a 4–6 inch gravel base add material and labor before any concrete is poured.
  • Reinforcement: Rebar or wire mesh for driveways and slabs on unstable soil adds cost but reduces cracking.
  • Finishing: Broom finish is cheapest; stamping, staining, or polishing adds $2–$8 per square foot.
  • Delivery: Long pump distances, remote sites, and minimum-load charges can push ready-mix prices higher.

Always collect two or three local quotes. Regional labor differences routinely swing the total more than the concrete itself.

Ways to keep slab costs down

  • Collect three quotes: Labor varies most by region, so competing bids reveal your real local price.
  • Keep the shape simple: Square and rectangular slabs waste less form material and labor than curves.
  • Choose a broom finish: Decorative stamping or staining can double the finishing cost for little structural gain.
  • Time your pour: Late spring and early fall are busy seasons; shoulder-season scheduling can lower labor rates.
  • Buy ready-mix above one yard: Once you pass roughly one cubic yard, delivered concrete almost always beats bagged.

Small savings on each of these add up. A $900 slab can often be brought under $750 with smart planning and a simple finish.

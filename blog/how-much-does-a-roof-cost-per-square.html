How Much Does a Roof Cost Per Square in 2026?

Published September 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Roofers price almost everything “per square.” A square is 100 square feet of actual roof surface, and every bundle of shingles, every roll of underlayment, and every labor hour is calculated against that 100-sq-ft unit. This guide breaks down what a roof costs per square in 2026 across the five most common US residential materials, what’s bundled into the per-square price, and how to convert it to a project total.

Quick answer: In 2026, installed roofing cost runs roughly $350–$450 per square for 3-tab asphalt, $450–$650 for architectural asphalt, $900–$1,400 for standing-seam metal, $1,000–$2,000 for clay or concrete tile, and $1,500–$3,000 for natural slate. A 17-square roof — the median US home — lands at $5,950–$51,000 depending on material.

What “per square” means

A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface — not 100 sq ft of home footprint. Because most roofs are pitched, the roof surface is always larger than the building footprint. A 1,700 sq ft single-story home with a 6/12 pitch has roughly 1,925 sq ft of roof surface, or about 19 squares. A simple gable two-story at the same footprint might have 17 squares.

The standard pitch multipliers for converting footprint to roof surface:

Pitch (rise / 12)Multiplier
Flat (0/12 – 2/12)1.00–1.02
Low (3/12 – 5/12)1.03–1.09
Standard (6/12 – 9/12)1.12–1.25
Steep (10/12 – 12/12)1.30–1.41
Very steep (14/12+)1.50+

These ranges reflect typical installed cost on a straightforward gable roof in a moderate-cost US market. Steeper pitches, complex geometry (multiple gables, dormers, valleys), two-story heights, and high-cost metro areas push the numbers upward by 10–40%.

What’s bundled into the per-square price

The “installed per square” price on a contractor’s quote bundles the major cost components into one line. Understanding what’s inside helps you compare bids apples-to-apples.

  • Materials: the shingles or panels, underlayment (synthetic or felt), ice & water shield at eaves and valleys, drip edge, flashing (pipe boots, chimney, valley), starter strip, ridge cap, and nails.
  • Labor: tear-off of one existing layer (if applicable), deck inspection, material hoist, install, cleanup, and haul-off of debris.
  • Disposal: landfill or roll-off fees for the torn-off shingles, typically $100–$300 per ton of debris.
  • Overhead and profit: contractor markup, generally 15–30% of direct cost.
  • Permit: $100–$600 depending on jurisdiction; sometimes itemized, sometimes folded in.

Items that aren’t bundled in the per-square price but frequently appear as separate lines: deck (sheathing) replacement, skylight replacement, chimney reflashing, gutter replacement, and structural repairs.

Why material matters most

Material choice is the single biggest cost driver and the longest-lasting decision in a re-roof. Here’s the practical comparison for a 17-square roof:

MaterialPer-square costLifespan25-yr cost (incl. one re-roof)
3-tab asphalt$350–$45015–25 yr$11,900–$15,300
Architectural asphalt$450–$65025–35 yr$7,650–$22,100
Standing-seam metal$900–$1,40040–70 yr$15,300–$23,800 (no second roof)

Cheaper materials cost less upfront but need replacement during a typical 30-year ownership. Architectural asphalt and metal both work out competitively over 30+ years when you include the avoided second re-roof.

Calculating your own per-square cost

  1. Measure the footprint of the house (length × width).
  2. Find your pitch and look up the multiplier above, or use a Roof Pitch Calculator.
  3. Multiply footprint by multiplier to get roof surface area in sq ft.
  4. Divide by 100 to convert to squares.
  5. Multiply squares by your chosen material’s per-square price to get the project total.
Project total ($) = (Footprint sq ft × Pitch multiplier / 100) × Per-square price

Worked example: a 1,700 sq ft single-story at 6/12 pitch, architectural shingles at $550/sq:

  • Roof surface: 1,700 × 1.12 = 1,904 sq ft
  • Squares: 1,904 / 100 = 19 squares
  • Project total: 19 × $550 = $10,450

What pushes the per-square price up or down

The per-square price isn’t fixed; it flexes with:

  • Roof complexity: multiple gables, dormers, valleys, and chimneys add cutting labor. A simple gable is the cheapest per square; a complex multi-gable can add 15–30%.
  • Pitch: anything above 8/12 slows crews and adds safety setup; very steep roofs (12/12+) often carry a 10–25% labor premium.
  • Height: two- and three-story roofs require material hoisting and longer ladder setup.
  • Access: tight urban lots with no driveway for the dump trailer pay more for disposal logistics.
  • Tear-off layers: a single layer is standard; a double layer adds labor and disposal cost (often $50–$100 per square extra).
  • Region: Northeast and West Coast metro areas run 20–35% above the national average.

How to read a contractor quote

A clean per-square quote lists the material, the squares, the per-square price, and the total. Watch for these hidden costs that show up mid-project:

  • Deck replacement at $70–$120 per 4×8 sheet of OSB or plywood, only known after tear-off.
  • Code upgrades like ice & water shield, additional ventilation, or new flashing required by current code but absent on the old roof.
  • Chimney reflashing at $300–$900 if the existing flashing is bad.
  • Skylight replacement at $500–$1,500 each, often opportunistic during a re-roof.

Get 3 written quotes with the same line items so the comparison is fair. A low per-square bid that omits tear-off, disposal, or flashing isn’t actually the cheapest.

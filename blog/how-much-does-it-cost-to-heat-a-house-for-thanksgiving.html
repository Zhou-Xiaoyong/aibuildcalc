How Much Does It Cost to Heat a House for Thanksgiving Guests?

Published November 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Eight guests for four days add about $19 to the gas and electric bill in a 1,800 sq ft Ohio house. The furnace accounts for $2.75 of it. Hot water, the oven, the dryer and one space heater in the spare bedroom make up the other $16.65. The bill is not the expensive part of Thanksgiving.

Quick answer: heating a house for Thanksgiving guests costs $15 to $30 in fuel and electricity for a four-day visit, depending on how many people shower and whether the guest room needs a space heater. A room that never gets above 62°F is the real line item: $25 to $1,900 to fix, or $2.16 a night to work around. The HVAC Cost Calculator prices equipment if the furnace is the thing that failed.

Thanksgiving is a four-day load, not a dinner

The house was sized for two people at 68°F. From Wednesday night to Sunday morning it holds eight, runs an oven for six hours, does four loads of laundry and opens the front door every time another car pulls in. None of that is dramatic on its own. Together it pushes the equipment past the point where it runs in the background.

It also lands in the week a heat pump starts calling for auxiliary heat in the low 30s.

Raising the thermostat is cheaper than most people expect, because heat loss scales with the gap between inside and outside, not with the number on the display.

Extra therms = UA x (new delta T - old delta T) x 24 / AFUE Whole house, UA = 520 BTU/h per degree F 68F inside, 40F outside: 520 x 28 x 24 = 349,440 BTU/day 72F inside, 40F outside: 520 x 32 x 24 = 399,360 BTU/day Difference: 49,920 BTU = 0.5 therm/day At 92% AFUE: 0.54 therm/day 4 days x 0.54 x $1.25 = $2.70 Hot water, 32 showers at 11 gal hot each: 352 gal x 8.34 lb x 65F rise = 190,800 BTU Tank at 0.60 EF = 3.2 therms = $4.00

The guest room is a separate problem

A 12 by 14 bonus room over the garage is the coldest square footage in most houses, and Thanksgiving is when somebody sleeps in it. Three exterior walls, a floor over an unheated slab, two windows, and a 6 in supply that was sized for a closet. At 70°F inside and 40°F outside the room needs about 5,100 BTU an hour. One 1,500 W heater delivers 5,120, which is why it runs flat out and the far wall still reads 64°F.

Do the arithmetic before buying a bigger heater. A second 1,500 W unit on the same 15 A circuit trips the breaker, and a 20 A circuit is a $350 electrician visit. The BTU Calculator gives the room load from dimensions and window count, and the room-by-room BTU guide explains the adjustments behind it.

Half the problem is usually air movement. A bedroom with a supply and no return pressurizes when the door closes, and a pressurized room stops accepting supply air. A 3/4 in undercut, or a transfer grille above the door, restores the path. That is a free fix with a circular saw, and the highest return in this article.

Attic work is priced per square foot of attic floor, not house. A 1,000 sq ft attic at R-49 runs $1.30 to $1.90 a square foot blown in, and the Insulation Calculator converts existing depth into bags and cost. On a 1,800 sq ft house burning 780 therms a season, R-19 to R-49 saves 60 to 90 therms, or $75 to $110 a year at Ohio rates. That is a five-year decision, not a Thanksgiving one.

What to do in the next 20 days

Work in this order. The cheap fixes change what the expensive ones need to be.

  • Close the guest room door and hold a tissue at the gap. If it flutters, cut the door or add a grille first.
  • Feel the floor over the garage on a cold morning. A cold floor means the joist bay is open to outside air, and no heater fixes that.
  • Make sure the supply in the room actually blows. A disconnected flex run in the attic is a 20 minute fix and a common one.
  • Set the thermostat to 68 on Wednesday and 66 overnight. Guests sleep better at 66 than at 74.
  • Buy one 1,500 W heater with a tip-over switch. It plugs into a wall outlet, never an extension cord.

If the spare room is also getting paint, do it now and not on the 24th. Latex needs 68°F to cure, and a cold room leaves a film that stays soft under furniture for a month. A 10x10 runs under $300 in material, per the room painting cost breakdown.

What not to do before Thursday

Do not close the register in the room you are trying to warm, and do not close every register to push heat upstairs. A furnace moves a fixed amount of air. Closing half the supplies raises static pressure until the blower moves less air everywhere and the limit switch starts cycling.

Do not use the oven as a heat source. Six hours with the door open puts carbon monoxide and about a pound of water vapor into the kitchen.

Thanksgiving heating questions

Is it cheaper to hold 72 all weekend or bump it Thursday morning?

Hold 68 and bump to 72 Wednesday night. Four days at 72 costs $2.75 more than 68. A setback to 62 saves about $1.40 a day, and recovery takes 45 to 90 minutes, so the savings survive it.

Why is the guest room 8 degrees colder than the hallway?

Usually three things at once: a supply sized for a closet, no return path once the door shuts, and a floor over an unheated garage. At 40°F outside the room needs roughly 30 BTU per square foot, and a 6 in supply at 100 CFM cannot carry it.

Does closing the vents in unused rooms save money?

Rarely more than a few dollars a month, and it costs comfort. Closed supplies pressurize those rooms and push air out through the same leaks that let cold air in. The Furnace Size Calculator is the better answer if the equipment is genuinely oversized.

The call

Spend $60 on a sweep and weatherstrip, cut the door bottom, and buy one good 1,500 W heater. That is $130 and it solves this Thanksgiving. Skip the attic and the knee walls unless the room gets used from December through March. If it does, the $1,600 knee-wall job pays back in about six years at Ohio gas rates and ends the space heater for good.

One Bathroom, 18 Thanksgiving Guests? 126 Visits, $8,900

Published November 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Eighteen people at one water closet is 126 visits on Thanksgiving Day and about 8.4 hours of a closed door, which is 53 percent of the hours that door is available. A 3 by 5 powder room ends that permanently for $8,900 and 21 working days. It will not be finished by the 26th. A restroom trailer or single unit delivered on Wednesday costs $185 to $350 and covers the same afternoon.

Quick answer: measure the closet before you call anybody. A powder room needs 30 inches of width and 21 inches of clear floor in front of the bowl, so a 3 by 5 space works and a 24-inch-deep bedroom closet does not. Load the new fixtures with the Plumbing Fixture Calculator and size the branch with the Drain Pipe Calculator before you ask for a quote. The drain, not the tile, decides whether this is a $4,800 job or an $8,900 one, and it is the only line on the estimate you cannot judge from the driveway.

The queue starts at 6:40 in the morning

Thanksgiving puts 18 people in a house built for four, and every one of them wants the same door. The arithmetic is not subtle. Adults use a bathroom six to eight times a day, call it seven, so the house generates 126 visits. At four minutes of occupancy each, that is 504 minutes, or 8.4 hours, out of the 16 hours the door is open.

Averages hide the morning.

About a fifth of those visits land between 8 and 10 a.m., which is 24 trips and 96 minutes of a 120-minute window. That is where the line forms on the stairs. The shower is a second constraint in the same room: 14 guests at eight minutes each is 112 minutes of running water behind the same locked latch, and the 50-gallon tank math is what fails first, not the drain.

A second fixture does not add luxury. It routes 63 visits to a different door, which drops single-bathroom occupancy from 53 percent to 26 and takes the 8-to-10 window from 96 occupied minutes to 48. That is the whole case, and it fits in one sentence you can say to your spouse.

Fifteen square feet, measured from the bowl out

A powder room is a toilet and a sink, and the code treats it as two clearances rather than a small bathroom. IRC R307.1 asks for 15 inches from the center of the bowl to any side wall and 21 inches of clear floor in front of it. R307.2 adds the part homeowners forget: the door cannot swing into either dimension.

Those two numbers set the floor at 30 inches wide by about 60 inches deep. A standard bedroom closet is 24 inches deep, which is six inches too shallow once a toilet and a door are in it, so the wall behind the closet moves into the closet or into the room next door. That framing is $700 to $1,100 before a plumber pulls up.

Wall length is the next squeeze. A toilet footprint is 30 inches wide, so a 24-inch vanity plus a 30-inch bowl is 54 inches of wall, and a 5-foot room has 60. A 30-inch vanity pushes the pair to exactly 60 with no gap for a towel bar. Check the fit with the Vanity Calculator before you fall for a 36-inch furniture piece in a photo.

Then the stack, which is the line item that changes everything. A 3-inch drain needs a 1-1/2-inch vent, and the vent either climbs to the roof or becomes an air admittance valve under IRC P3114 if your jurisdiction permits one. A roof penetration runs $600 to $900 with flashing and a roofer. An AAV is a $60 part and one conversation with the inspector. Ask which one you are allowed before you design around it.

What eight days will buy

Nothing structural that starts today finishes by the 26th. A permit takes three to five working days in most jurisdictions, and the rough inspection has to happen before drywall, so the clock is not the contractor's to compress.

So there are two decisions this week, not one.

This year: rent a unit for $185 to $350, place it on a level pad with a clear delivery path, and put a shower schedule on the fridge starting at 6:30. Total cost under $400, and it holds up for four days of 18 people.

Next year: book the powder room for February. Permits are quicker in January, contractors have crews, and tile selection is not happening in a parking lot the week of the holiday. The trade-off is honest. A February build costs the same $8,900 and improves exactly zero of this year's mornings.

Thanksgiving bathroom questions

Can a toilet go under the stairs?

Yes, if the space is 30 inches wide and the drain can fall a quarter inch per foot to the stack. Headroom is not the code issue under a stair; the fixture clearances and the vent are. Measure the width at the narrowest point, which is usually the door frame, not the room.

Does a half bath pay for itself?

It shows up in an appraisal as a change in bathroom count, which is one of the few line items an appraiser cannot argue with. Published cost-recouped figures for mid-range bath work land near 60 percent, and those figures do not include the 63 visits you move off the only door in the house.

Will an air admittance valve pass inspection?

Under IRC P3114, one AAV per fixture, installed accessibly and above the trap weir. Some jurisdictions still want a true vent to the roof, and a few require the AAV to be in a ventilated cabinet. This is a two-minute question at the counter and a $600 answer if you guess wrong.

The call

If the closet shares a wall with the existing bath, call a plumber on Monday and build it. $4,200 to $6,500, six to nine working days, and a permit pulled this week can still clear the 26th. That is the rare version of this job that fits the calendar.

If the drain has to cross a slab, rent for $260 this year and schedule the build for February. Breaking concrete in the week of Thanksgiving buys you a hole in the floor and a house full of guests, and the $8,900 is the same money in March as it is in November.

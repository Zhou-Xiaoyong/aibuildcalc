Cost to Paint Interior of a 2000 Sq Ft House

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Two thousand square feet is the most common size in American residential construction, and it is the size homeowners ask about most when they call a painter. The quote for the same house can land anywhere from about $3,800 to $8,500, and the gap is almost never about the paint itself. It is about ceiling height, how much trim and millwork the house carries, how many colours are involved, and whether the rooms are empty when the crew arrives.

Quick answer: Professional interior painting for a 2,000 sq ft house typically runs $1.90 to $4.25 per square foot of floor area, which works out to roughly $3,800 to $8,500 for walls, ceilings and standard trim. Most single-storey jobs land near $5,500. Put your own room count and ceiling height into the Painting Cost Calculator for a tighter number.

Why floor area is a proxy, not a measurement

Paint goes on walls and ceilings, so a square-foot price based on floor area only works because house layouts are fairly predictable. In a typical 2,000 sq ft single-storey home, paintable wall area lands between 4,800 and 5,600 square feet once interior partitions are counted, and the ceilings add another 2,000. That is roughly 7,000 square feet of coated surface before trim.

At a realistic 350 to 400 square feet per gallon per coat, a whole-house job needs around 18 to 20 gallons per coat, or 36 to 40 gallons for two coats. That gallon count is the number to sanity-check any quote against, because paint is the one line item a contractor cannot hide.

Wall area (sq ft) ≈ floor area (sq ft) x 2.4 to 2.8 Ceiling area (sq ft) = floor area (sq ft) Trim allowance = about 8% to 12% of wall plus ceiling area Gallons per coat = total paintable sq ft / 375

Room by room, where the money actually goes

Contractors price room by room because cut-in time, masking and furniture protection scale with the number of rooms rather than with raw square footage. A small bathroom can cost more per square foot than a large bedroom for exactly that reason.

Professional versus DIY for the same house

Doing it yourself does not remove the material cost, and on a house this size the material bill is substantial. Two coats over 7,000 square feet needs 36 to 40 gallons of finish paint at roughly $35 to $70 per gallon, plus primer and supplies. The saving is real, but so is the time: a first-time painter should budget 80 to 120 hours for a full interior, spread over several weekends and a great deal of furniture moving.

The five things that move a 2,000 sq ft quote

Ceiling height. Anything above nine feet adds ladders, staging or scaffolding, and most crews price that as a surcharge rather than absorbing it. Colour changes. Going from a dark saturated colour to off-white can require a third coat or a tinted primer, which is a material and labour increase on every wall. Trim and doors. Doors are usually priced per side at $100 to $150, and a house with extensive crown, wainscoting or window casings can add 15 to 25 percent. Surface repairs. Nail pops, drywall patches, caulking failures and popcorn ceiling removal are separate scopes. Occupancy. An empty house is faster and cheaper; moving and protecting furniture is often billed as a day of labour.

What a written estimate should itemise

Ask for the room list with the number of coats per surface, the product name and sheen, the amount of trim measured in linear feet, a drywall repair allowance stated in dollars, and the cleanup terms. A quote that says only "interior repaint, two coats, $6,200" is not comparable to anything. If you want to check the surface area behind the number yourself, the Paint Calculator converts room dimensions into gallons, and the painting tool collection covers ceilings, primer and exterior work separately.

Getting three quotes that are actually comparable

Write a one-page scope sheet first: rooms, surfaces, colours, sheen, whether ceilings are included, and who moves the furniture. Send the same sheet to all three contractors. The bids will still differ, but they will differ on labour rate and overhead rather than on scope, which is the only difference worth negotiating. Expect the middle quote to be the realistic one, and treat a quote 30 percent below the others as a scope problem rather than a bargain.

Concrete Block Wall Cost Calculator

Published August 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Pricing a concrete block wall comes down to three quantities: how many blocks, how much mortar, and how much grout goes into the cores. Get those right and the cost estimate follows directly. This guide gives you the per-square-foot factors that masons actually use, the 2026 material and labor ranges, and a worked example for a typical garden or retaining wall.

Quick answer: A standard 8x8x16 CMU wall needs 1.125 blocks per square foot of wall face and about 3 bags of mortar per 100 blocks. Installed cost runs roughly $12 to $28 per square foot in 2026. Get exact counts from the Concrete Block Calculator.

Block count: the 1.125 factor

A standard concrete masonry unit is nominally 8 inches high by 16 inches long, which includes the 3/8-inch mortar joint. That face measures 8 x 16 = 128 square inches, or 0.889 square feet. Inverting gives the factor every mason keeps in their head:

Wall area (sq ft) = Length (ft) x Height (ft) Blocks needed = Wall area x 1.125 Order quantity = Blocks needed x 1.05 [5% breakage allowance] Example: 40 ft long x 6 ft high garden wall Area = 240 sq ft Blocks = 240 x 1.125 = 270 Order = 270 x 1.05 = 284 blocks

Subtract openings over about 10 square feet, but do not bother subtracting small ones — the cutting waste around an opening roughly cancels the blocks you save. For 12-inch-high blocks the factor drops to 0.75 per square foot; for 4-inch-high units it rises to 2.25.

Whether you grout every cell or only the reinforced ones is the single biggest swing in material cost. A fully grouted 8-inch wall uses roughly four times the concrete of a partially grouted one. Retaining walls and anything load-bearing generally require more grouting; a freestanding garden wall usually does not.

Individual standard grey blocks run about $2.00 to $3.50 each in 2026, with split-face and colored units at $3.50 to $6.50. Masonry labor is typically $50 to $95 per hour, and an experienced mason lays roughly 90 to 130 standard blocks per day on a straightforward wall.

Worked cost example

Taking the 40 x 6 ft garden wall from above, partially grouted:

Blocks 284 @ $2.75 = $ 781 Mortar 9 bags @ $11 = $ 99 Grout 0.6 cu yd @ $195 = $ 117 Rebar #4 ~96 lin ft @ $1.10 = $ 106 Footing 40 x 1.5 x 0.67 ft = 1.5 cu yd @ $180 = $ 270 -------------------------------------------- Material subtotal = $1,373 Labor 240 sq ft @ $9/sq ft = $2,160 -------------------------------------------- TOTAL installed (~$14.7 / sq ft) = $3,533

The footing is easy to overlook. A block wall over about 3 feet tall needs a continuous footing roughly twice the wall width and below local frost depth, and that concrete is a real line item.

Block vs. brick vs. poured concrete

Block is usually the cheapest masonry option per square foot of wall because the units are large and go up fast. Brick costs more in both material and labor since you are laying roughly seven units per square foot instead of just over one — the brick calculator shows exactly how the counts diverge. A poured concrete wall is competitive on tall structural walls where forming can be reused, but on short residential runs the forming cost dominates and block wins. For freestanding piers and columns along a block wall, size those separately with the Concrete Column Calculator.

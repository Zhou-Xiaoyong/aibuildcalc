Subfloor Replacement Cost Per Square Foot

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Eight hundred square feet of rotted plywood in a 1970s ranch prices out near $4,600 when the joists underneath are sound, and about $7,400 once two of them get sistered. The panels are roughly $1,500 of either number. The rest is tear-out, disposal and the joist work nobody sees once the floor is closed up.

Quick answer: Subfloor replacement costs $3 to $8 per square foot installed in 2026 when the joists are sound. A whole floor re-sheeted in 23/32 in tongue-and-groove plywood lands at $4.50 to $7.00 per sq ft. Patching 40 sq ft prices at $450 to $900 because most crews carry a minimum. The Subfloor Calculator converts room dimensions into sheets, screws and a material estimate.

Installed cost per square foot, by scope

Every bid here is two numbers stacked. Tear-out and disposal run $0.75 to $1.50 per sq ft over carpet or vinyl, and closer to $2.00 where thinset has to be chipped off tile. Panel, adhesive, screws and labor run $2.25 to $4.50. Anything under 100 sq ft picks up a $450 to $900 minimum, so a powder room patch reads $11 per sq ft on paper and $6 per sq ft inside a larger job.

Those are national bands for 2026. A 23/32 in T&G sheet is $42 to $68 depending on region, and crews in the Northeast and coastal markets price installation 20 to 40 percent above the Midwest. The same 800 sq ft floor quotes at $3,600 in Ohio and $5,300 in Massachusetts.

The panel math behind the quote

A 4 by 8 sheet covers 32 sq ft. Waste runs about 5 percent, not the 10 percent a flooring carton needs, because offcuts from the last row go into closets and toe kicks instead of the trash.

Sheets = (room sq ft x 1.05) / 32 Adhesive = 1 tube per 2 sheets, 10 oz construction adhesive Screws = 1-1/4 in coated deck screws, about 30 per sheet Joist bays = room width / 16 in, rounded up Worked example, 14 ft x 20 ft room, 23/32 in T&G plywood: 14 x 20 = 280 sq ft 280 x 1.05 = 294 sq ft 294 / 32 = 9.2 -> order 10 sheets 10 x 32 = 320 sq ft on hand Same room at $58 a sheet, Northeast pricing: 10 x 58 = $580 in plywood screws and adhesive = $85 tear-out at $1.20 per sq ft = $336

Tongue-and-groove panels are the default on a floor. Square-edge panels need solid blocking at every seam, and that blocking costs more in labor than the T&G premium costs at the counter.

Plywood, OSB, or a coated panel

Bare OSB at $30 to $46 a sheet is the cheapest way to close a floor, and it is the panel that swells at a wet edge and stays swollen. Plywood at $42 to $68 holds a screw after a leak and takes a patch without crumbling. Coated OSB at $58 to $80 earns its price in a bathroom or a laundry room.

Five plies in a 23/32 in panel make a stiffer floor than four, and a core with visible voids will telegraph through vinyl and crack a grout line within two seasons.

What the inspector will look at

Panel thickness is set by joist spacing, not by what is in stock. IRC Table R503.2.1(1) allows 5/8 in over joists at 16 in on center and calls for 3/4 in at 24 in. Screws go 6 in apart on edges and 12 in in the field, and they have to bite into the joist.

Deflection decides whether tile belongs on top. Ceramic needs L/360 and stone needs L/720, and a floor that flexes under a heel will crack grout no matter how thick the panel. The Floor Joist Calculator checks a span against those limits before anyone buys a sheet.

Leave a 1/8 in gap between panels. Tight panels buckle into a ridge in August, and that ridge rocks every seam of a floating floor.

Bid A is not a scam. The 7/16 in panel meets code on 16 in centers, and a homeowner selling in two years can live with it. What it cannot do is carry tile, and the soft joist stays soft. Bid B costs $3,640 more and buys a floor that will not move.

What to buy

For an 800 sq ft main floor over joists that pass the deflection check, buy 23/32 in T&G plywood, glue it and screw it, and budget $4,600 to $5,200 installed with tear-out. A 5/8 in panel with floor leveler over it saves about $900 and gives up the stiffness that keeps tile flat.

Expect a finished floor about 3/4 in higher than the old one. That means door undercuts at $25 to $50 each, a toilet flange extension at $30 to $75, and a transition strip at every doorway.

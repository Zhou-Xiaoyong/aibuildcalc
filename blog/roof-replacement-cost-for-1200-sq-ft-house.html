Roof Replacement Cost for a 1,200 Sq Ft House in 2026

Published September 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 1,200 sq ft house sits well below the US median home size, which is why roof replacement quotes on these homes often look surprisingly affordable. The catch: smaller roofs still pay many of the same fixed costs as larger ones — permits, dump fees, setup labor, and minimum order charges — so the per-square price on a 1,200 sq ft home runs 10–20% higher than on a 2,000 sq ft home. This guide walks through a worked 2026 cost example and explains the levers that move the total.

Quick answer: Replacing the roof on a 1,200 sq ft home in 2026 costs roughly $5,500–$9,500 for architectural asphalt on a simple gable, $4,500–$7,500 for 3-tab asphalt, and $10,500–$17,000 for standing-seam metal. Region and roof complexity move the number by 20–40%. Use our Roofing Calculator for an estimate from your exact footprint and pitch.

Step 1 — Convert footprint to squares

A 1,200 sq ft house has a smaller roof surface than the name suggests once you account for pitch. Use the same multipliers as larger homes:

Roof surface (sq ft) = Footprint sq ft × Pitch multiplier Squares = Roof surface (sq ft) / 100

For a typical 1,200 sq ft single-story ranch with a 6/12 pitch:

  • Roof surface: 1,200 × 1.12 = 1,344 sq ft
  • Squares: 1,344 / 100 = ~13.5 squares

For a simple two-story with the same footprint at a steeper 8/12 pitch:

  • Roof surface: 1,200 × 1.20 = 1,440 sq ft = ~14.5 squares

For a low-slope 3/12 ranch:

  • Roof surface: 1,200 × 1.03 = 1,236 sq ft = ~12.5 squares

A useful rule of thumb: a 1,200 sq ft home almost always falls between 12 and 16 roofing squares, depending on story count and pitch. For the exact number on a specific home, use the Roof Pitch Calculator.

Step 2 — Choose material

Material is the single biggest cost driver. For a 14-square roof on a 1,200 sq ft home, here are typical 2026 totals in a moderate-cost US market:

Step 3 — Apply the small-roof premium

Roofers price by the square, but the fixed costs (permits, dump fees, setup, insurance verification) barely change with project size. On a 14-square roof those fixed costs are spread across fewer squares, pushing the effective per-square price up. Expect:

  • 14 squares (1,200 sq ft home): 10–20% per-square premium vs. a 17–22 square roof.
  • 10 squares or fewer: minimum trip-charge territory; expect the highest per-square premium, sometimes 25–40%.

In practice, a $550/sq architectural quote on a 1,700 sq ft home (17 squares) might land at $600–$650/sq on the same exact shingle when the home is 1,200 sq ft (14 squares).

Step 4 — Layer in regional cost

Where the home sits matters as much as the material choice. The 2026 national average cost of a roof replacement is about $8,500, but state and regional multipliers push that around significantly. For a 1,200 sq ft home:

Region (typical 1,200 sq ft, architectural)14-square total
Sun Belt / South (TX, FL, GA, AL, MS, NC)$5,500–$7,500
Midwest (OH, IN, MI, MO, IA)$6,500–$8,500
Mountain West (CO, AZ, NV, UT)$7,000–$9,500
Northeast (NY, NJ, MA, PA, CT)$8,500–$11,500

Hawaii / Alaska$11,000–$15,500

These ranges assume architectural shingles, a simple gable roof, single-layer tear-off, and standard code-compliant underlayment and flashing. State-specific numbers are in our 2026 Roof Replacement Costs by State report.

Step 5 — Add the line items that aren’t in the per-square price

Most contractors quote the per-square price as the headline number, but the final invoice usually adds line items. On a 1,200 sq ft home, budget for these even if they don’t appear on the initial quote:

  • Permit: $150–$500 depending on jurisdiction.
  • Tear-off of one existing layer: usually included; a second layer adds $50–$100 per square.
  • Deck (sheathing) replacement: $70–$120 per 4×8 sheet if soft spots are found. Contingency for 5–15 sheets is realistic on a 20+ year old roof: $400–$1,800.
  • Chimney reflashing: $300–$900 if the existing flashing is failing.
  • Skylight reflashing or replacement: $300–$1,500 each.
  • Gutter and downspout replacement: $5–$10 per linear foot if it’s time; usually a separate quote.
  • Upgrade to ice & water shield: $1–$2 per sq ft if required by code (cold climates) or for homeowner preference.

A reasonable contingency on a 1,200 sq ft re-roof is 10–15% of the contract value, or roughly $700–$1,400.

Worked total for a typical 1,200 sq ft ranch

Putting it together for a single-story, 6/12 pitch ranch with architectural shingles, single-layer tear-off, in a moderate-cost Midwest market:

Roof surface: 1,200 sq ft × 1.12 = 1,344 sq ft (~13.5 squares) Per-square price: $550 (architectural, Midwest, small-roof premium) Roofing subtotal: 13.5 × $550 = $7,425 Permit: $250 Drip edge, ice & water shield at eaves, valley flashing: bundled in per-square Deck contingency (8 sheets of OSB): 8 × $90 = $720 Total estimated project: ~$8,400

The same home in California at the same pitch with the same material would land closer to $11,000–$12,500; in West Virginia or Mississippi, closer to $6,500–$7,500.

How to get a tighter number

  1. Measure your roof from the ground or with a satellite estimate (Google Earth, HOVER, EagleView).
  2. Pick a material based on budget, climate, and aesthetic.
  3. Apply the right pitch multiplier to convert footprint to squares.
  4. Multiply by your region’s typical per-square price (the regional table above is a starting point).
  5. Add 10–15% contingency for deck, flashing, and permit unknowns.

The Roofing Calculator walks through these steps automatically and applies a state cost multiplier when you share your zip code.

Three things to verify on any quote

  1. Tear-off, disposal, and permit are itemized. A low per-square price that omits these isn’t actually low.
  2. Underlayment and flashing are specified by type. Synthetic felt and ice & water shield at eaves should be on the quote.
  3. Workmanship warranty is at least 2 years (5+ years is reasonable for established contractors). Manufacturer’s material warranty is separate.

Get 3 written quotes that all use the same line items. Compare apples to apples on material grade, tear-off layers, deck replacement allowance, and warranty terms.

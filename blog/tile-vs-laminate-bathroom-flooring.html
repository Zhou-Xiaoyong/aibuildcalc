Tile vs Laminate Bathroom Flooring

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 5 by 8 bathroom is 40 sq ft. Porcelain tile prices at $380 to $720 installed; a 12 mm laminate lands at $180 to $320. The laminate saves about $300 the day it goes down. Whether that is a bargain depends on one question: does this room have a tub or a shower?

Quick answer: Tile costs $7.50 to $20 per square foot installed in 2026 and laminate $3.50 to $8.00. In a full bath, tile wins on water alone: most laminate warranties exclude rooms with a tub or shower. Laminate suits a powder room, where 40 sq ft saves $300 to $400. Installed cost per square foot, by product

A bathroom floor bid is two lines, material and labor. The gap opens in labor. A tile setter bills $6 to $13 per sq ft in most metros; a laminate installer bills $2 to $4. Both carry a $400 to $700 minimum, so a small bath reads pricier per square foot than a 900 sq ft basement.

Those are national 2026 bands. Metro labor in the Northeast, Chicago and coastal California runs 25 to 40 percent above the Midwest, so the same porcelain floor bids near $900 in Boston and $560 in Columbus. Cement board and thinset add $200 to $280 per tile line.

Water is the whole argument

Laminate is a wood-fiber core under a printed image and a wear layer. The wear layer handles a mop. The core does not handle a puddle that sits. A supply line dripping behind a toilet for a week swells the HDF into a ridge, and no sander fixes that because there is no veneer left to sand.

Water-resistant laminate, with sealed edges and a tight-lock joint, buys hours rather than days. It is not waterproof: most manufacturers void coverage for a bathroom with a tub or shower.

Porcelain has no core to swell. Impervious tile under ANSI A137.1 absorbs under 0.5 percent of its weight, and over a waterproofed substrate it outlasts every fixture on it.

The tile math for a 40 sq ft floor

Tile sells by coverage, not by piece. A 12 by 24 in tile is 2 sq ft, so a box of six covers 12 sq ft, and that printed number builds the order. Waste runs 10 percent in a square room and 15 percent with a tub alcove and a door jog.

Boxes = (floor sq ft x 1.10) / box coverage Thinset = 1 bag, 50 lb, per 60 to 80 sq ft Backer board = floor sq ft / 3, plus 10 percent for cuts Worked example, 5x8 bathroom, 12x24 porcelain at 12 sq ft per box: 5 x 8 = 40 sq ft 40 x 1.10 = 44 sq ft 44 / 12 = 3.7 -> order 4 boxes (48 sq ft) Materials for that floor: 48 sq ft x $5.50 = $264 tile thinset = $28 1/4 in cement board, 14 sheets at $14 = $196 total = $510 with grout

That list is why a 40 sq ft tile floor still bids at $700 to $900 with the tile under $300.

Height is what owners notice later. Tile over 1/4 in cement board lifts the surface 3/8 in, so the flange needs an extension ring at $10 to $30 and the door needs an undercut at $25 to $50. Laminate at 8 mm plus 2 mm underlayment lifts 5/16 in and clears the door swing.

Subfloor, deflection and the flange

Tile over wood needs a stiff floor, and that is a specification, not a preference. TCNA method F144 calls for a 1-1/8 in assembly, which on a 3/4 in subfloor means adding 3/8 in plywood or an uncoupling membrane. Ceramic needs L/360 and stone L/720. The Floor Joist Calculator checks a span against both before anyone orders a box.

Laminate asks less of thickness and more of flatness: 3/16 in over 10 ft. A 1950s bath with a crowned joist will rock a floating floor until a locking tab snaps.

The flange is the line nobody budgets. A cast iron flange set at the old floor height sits 3/8 in below new tile, and the fix is an extension ring or a thicker wax ring. Finding out after the grout cures is what costs.

What to buy

For a bathroom with a tub or a shower, buy porcelain and skip the comparison. A matte 12 by 24 porcelain at $3 to $6 per sq ft over 1/4 in cement board, with a waterproof membrane in the wet zone, budgets $600 to $900 installed. The trade-off is downtime: the toilet comes out and the room sits two days.

Save laminate for a powder room or a basement bath whose shower sees a guest twice a year, where 40 sq ft at $180 to $320 buys a floor due in 15 years instead of 35.

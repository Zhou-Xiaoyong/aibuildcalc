Painting the Guest Room Before Thanksgiving: 3 Gallons, $212, 14 Days

Published November 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 12 by 14 guest room has 365 square feet of paintable wall once the door and two windows come out. Two coats is 730 square feet, which at 375 square feet per gallon is 1.95 gallons, or 3 gallons on the invoice after waste and a spare for touch-ups. Material runs $212. A painter quotes $420 to $700 for the same room. The gap is not paint. It is eleven hours of your evenings and a cure clock that started ticking two weeks ago.

Quick answer: paint it if the walls are scuffed or freshly patched, skip it if they are only dirty. A full two-coat job is 3 gallons of wall paint, 1 gallon of ceiling flat, 1 quart of trim enamel, $212 in material, and about 11 hours spread over three evenings. Start by November 19. That leaves seven days for the film to cure and the odor to leave before someone sleeps in there. The takeoff, measured on the wall, not the floor

Floor area is what paint is sold against at the counter and it is the wrong number for the job. A 12 by 14 room is 168 square feet of floor and 416 square feet of gross wall at an 8 foot ceiling. Perimeter is 52 feet. Multiply by height and you have the wall.

Then subtract the openings, which is where most estimates drift. A standard 3 by 7 door is 21 square feet. Two 3 by 5 windows are 30 more. That leaves 365 square feet of actual surface. A closet with bifold doors does not get deducted, because those doors get painted too.

Coverage is the other half of the math. A gallon of interior latex lays down 350 to 400 square feet per coat on a smooth primed wall, and 200 to 300 on bare drywall that drinks the first coat. The coverage breakdown explains why the same can cover half as much on a porous surface. Use 375 as a working number and you will be close on a previously painted wall.

Two details change the order by a gallon. A room painted a dark color needs a tinted primer coat or a third finish coat, and a room with heavy texture such as knockdown absorbs 10 to 20 percent more. Both are reasons to buy the third gallon rather than drive back on a Sunday.

Three routes, and what each one really costs

The decision in mid November is not which color. It is whether this room gets a full repaint, a scrub and touch-up, or a check written to somebody else.

Read the second row before the first. A room that is merely dingy does not need two coats of paint, it needs a wash and a quart. Fingerprints, scuff marks around the switch plate and the gray band where the vacuum hits the baseboard all come off with a sponge and a $12 bottle of TSP substitute. That is the correct answer for a room whose paint is otherwise sound, and it costs a tenth of the repaint.

The third row is where the pricing gets strange. Painters price a single room against a minimum, because the setup, the plastic and the second trip cost the same whether the room is 168 square feet or 300. That is why the site's interior cost per square foot runs $1.50 to $3.50 and a one-room job often lands at the top of that band rather than the middle.

The cure clock that decides your start date

Latex paint is dry to the touch in an hour and recoatable in four. Neither of those is the number that matters when somebody is going to sleep in the room. The film keeps releasing solvent for days, and a freshly painted bedroom smells like a paint store for the first 72 hours and faintly for a week after that.

Count backward from November 26. A start date of November 15 gives eleven days of cure, which is past the point where a low-VOC wall coating stops off-gassing noticeably. November 22 gives four days, which is enough for the paint to be hard but not enough for the smell to leave a small room with the door shut.

Recoating too early is the failure that shows up in photographs. Four hours is the label minimum at 70°F and 50 percent humidity, and it stretches to six or eight in a cold room. Pull a roller across paint that is still soft and it lifts, leaving a lap line that no amount of extra paint will hide. Wait the full four hours even when the wall looks dry.

Why the bonus room over the garage is the hard one

Most guest rooms that get painted in November are the room nobody uses, and in a lot of houses that is the 12 by 14 bonus room over the garage. It is also the coldest square footage in the building, which turns a paint label into a constraint. Interior latex is specified for application between 50°F and 85°F, and the film needs to stay above roughly 50 for the first 48 hours while it coalesces.

Below that, the binder stops knitting together. The coat looks fine on the second day and then powders off under a finger a month later, and the only fix is to sand it back and start over. If that room drops to 55°F overnight with the supply register choked down, cracking the window for ventilation is the wrong move on a 30°F night.

Fix the heat first if you are painting that room. The Thanksgiving heating cost breakdown walks through why a 1,500 watt heater is what keeps that room at 68, and the guest bedroom cost guide covers the larger version of the same problem if the room is not a bedroom yet.

What to buy, in the order you will use it

  • Joint compound and a sanding sponge. Two thin coats on nail pops and corner dings, 24 hours apart. Compound needs the same 50°F floor as paint, and it shrinks, so thin beats thick.
  • PVA primer for the patches. Bare compound absorbs the finish coat differently and shows as a flat spot under a sheen. A quart is $16 and it is the difference between invisible and obvious.
  • One gallon of ceiling flat. Ceilings are painted one coat when the color has not changed, and flat hides the roller texture that eggshell exaggerates.
  • Three gallons of wall paint in eggshell. Eggshell is scrubable, which matters in a room that gets used four days a year and cleaned once. Buy all three in the same base at the same time; a later gallon from a different batch can read a shade off.
  • A 2.5 inch angled sash brush. Cutting in takes longer than rolling and it is the part that shows. Cheap bristles leave brush marks along the ceiling line.
  • 9 inch roller, 3/8 inch nap, two covers. A 3/8 nap is right for smooth drywall. A 1/2 inch nap on a smooth wall leaves stipple you will see at a low sun angle.
  • Frog tape, canvas drop cloth, a tube of paintable caulk. Caulk the baseboard and casing gaps before painting. It is a 20 minute job that makes a 15 year old room look rebuilt.

Skip the paint-and-primer-in-one if the walls are bare or patched. It is a finish coat with extra solids, not a substitute for a primer coat on bare gypsum, and it costs $8 more a gallon to do a job it was not formulated for.

Thanksgiving paint questions

Can guests sleep in a room painted two days ago?

They can, and they will notice. Latex is dry to the touch in an hour and safe to be around, but the film still releases solvent and the smell lingers in a closed room for three to five days. If the start date slipped past November 22, use a low-VOC product under 50 grams per liter, keep a window cracked during the day, and put a box fan in the doorway pulling air out of the room rather than into it.

Is one coat enough if I use a paint and primer combo?

Over an existing painted wall in a similar color, one coat of a quality eggshell covers. Over a patch, a color change, or bare drywall, it does not, and the thin spots show as lighter streaks under a window. Two coats on 365 square feet costs one extra gallon and about 90 minutes. The shortcut is not worth the visible difference in a room you will look at every holiday for the next decade.

Should I paint the ceiling too, or just the walls?

Paint the ceiling if it is yellowed, water stained, or more than fifteen years old. A fresh white ceiling is the single biggest change in how a small room reads, and it costs $30 and one hour with an extension pole. Leave it if it is clean and white; overhead work on a ladder in a furnished room is where backs get hurt and where paint lands on the bed.

The call

Wash the walls tonight, look at them in daylight tomorrow, and decide from there. If what you see is dirt, spend $42 on TSP substitute and a quart of matched paint and put the other $170 into a better mattress for the guest bed. If what you see is scuffed paint, patched drywall and a ceiling that has gone the color of old paper, buy the three gallons and start by the 15th.

The trade-off is honest. Eleven hours of your own evenings saves about $348 against the middle of a painter's range, and you will see roller lap marks at a low angle in daylight for the first month because a first-time two-coat job rarely lays perfectly flat. A crew at $560 gets it flat, gets the ceiling included, and is in and out in a day. In a room you are painting once, that is money well spent. In a room you will repaint in four years anyway, it is not.

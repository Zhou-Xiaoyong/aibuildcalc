Metal Roof Cost Per Square Foot in 2026

Published September 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Metal roofing is the longest-lasting residential roof most US homeowners can buy, and the price gap inside the category is larger than in any other roofing material. A through-fastened steel panel and a 24-gauge standing-seam roof can differ by more than double per square foot. This guide breaks down what metal actually costs per square foot in 2026 and how to read a quote.

Quick answer: In 2026, installed metal roofing runs about $5–$9 per sq ft for exposed-fastener steel panels and $9–$15 per sq ft for standing seam. Copper and zinc run much higher. On a 1,900 sq ft roof that is roughly $9,500–$28,500 before tear-off. Use our Metal Roofing Calculator for panel counts and a project total.

The material-versus-labor split

Homeowners often assume metal is expensive because the material is expensive. In practice, labor and fabrication account for roughly half of a standing-seam invoice:

  • Panel material: typically $2.50–$5.00 per sq ft for painted steel, more for aluminum and far more for copper. Wider panels reduce the number of seams and the labor per square foot.
  • Labor: roughly $4–$8 per sq ft for standing seam, because panels are long, easily dented, and installed with concealed clips that must be aligned precisely.
  • Trim, flashing, and closures: usually 10–15% of the total. Valleys, chimneys, and complex transitions drive this line up quickly.
  • Tear-off and disposal: billed separately. Removing one asphalt layer typically costs $1.00–$1.50 per sq ft.

Why standing seam costs more than exposed fastener

The difference is not marketing. Standing-seam panels attach with concealed clips, so no fastener penetrates the roof surface, and the raised seam allows the metal to expand and contract with temperature without working the fasteners loose. That is why standing seam carries much longer warranties and is the default choice where snow, wind, or hail is a factor.

Through-fastened panels are screwed directly through the metal into the deck. They are cheaper and perfectly serviceable on simple roofs, but the exposed washer screws are the wear point and need periodic inspection.

Project cost = (Roof area in sq ft × installed cost per sq ft) + Tear-off and disposal + Trim, flashing and closures + Permit Note: Roof area means roof surface area, not footprint. Multiply footprint by the pitch multiplier first.

Worked example: a 1,700 sq ft home at 6/12 pitch

  • Roof surface: 1,700 sq ft footprint × 1.12 pitch multiplier = 1,904 sq ft
  • Divide by 100: about 19 squares
  • Standing seam, 24 ga, at $12 per sq ft: 1,904 × $12 = $22,848
  • Through-fastened panel at $7 per sq ft: 1,904 × $7 = $13,328
  • Tear-off of one asphalt layer at $1.25 per sq ft: about $2,380 additional

So the realistic range for this home is roughly $15,700 for a budget metal system and $25,200 for a mid-grade standing-seam roof. What moves the number

  • Gauge: 24-gauge steel costs noticeably more than 26-gauge and resists hail and foot traffic far better. Going up a gauge is usually money well spent.
  • Finish: a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) paint system such as Kynar 500 or Hylar 5000 holds color far longer than a polyester finish and adds to the price.
  • Panel length: panels run in one piece from eave to ridge where possible. Long runs mean fewer end laps and fewer leak points, but they need a roll former on site or careful freight handling.
  • Pitch and complexity: steep slopes slow the crew; valleys, dormers, and multiple roof planes multiply trim work.
  • Code requirements: high-wind and heavy-snow regions require closer clip spacing and heavier panels.

If you plan to stay in the home, the metal premium is usually repaid. If you are selling within five years, you will not recover the full cost.

How to compare metal roof quotes

Metal quotes are difficult to compare because “metal roof” describes a dozen different products. Ask every contractor the same questions:

  • What gauge is the panel, and what is the panel profile and width?
  • What finish system is used, and what is the finish warranty period?
  • What is the clip spacing, and does it meet the wind uplift requirement for this address?
  • Is tear-off, disposal, and deck repair included or billed separately?

A quote that omits tear-off, trim, or deck repair is not cheaper; it is incomplete. Compare at least three bids on identical line items, and if the roof is flat or low-slope, weigh the metal system against the membrane options in our flat roof vs pitched roof cost comparison before you commit.

How Many Bundles of Shingles Per Square?

Published September 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Almost every asphalt roof in the United States is ordered in bundles, quoted in squares, and installed by the square. Those are not the same unit, and confusing them is how homeowners end up a pallet short on install day. This guide explains the 3-bundle rule, the products that break it, and how to add waste before you order.

Quick answer: For nearly all standard 3-tab and architectural asphalt shingles, 3 bundles cover 1 square (100 sq ft of roof surface). Premium, designer, and some impact-rated products take 4 bundles per square. Always order about 10% extra. What a “square” actually covers

A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface — not 100 sq ft of house footprint. Because a roof is pitched, its surface is always larger than the floor plan beneath it. A 1,700 sq ft single-story home at a 6/12 pitch has roughly 1,900 sq ft of roof, which is about 19 squares. A two-story home of the same footprint with a steeper roof can exceed 22 squares.

If you are starting from the footprint rather than the roof surface, get the pitch multiplier first with our Roof Pitch Calculator.

Why the 3-bundle rule exists

Manufacturers package shingles so that three bundles lay out exactly one square of finished coverage. Each bundle covers about 33.3 square feet. The arithmetic works because shingle courses overlap: the installed area a bundle covers is smaller than the physical surface area of the material inside it. The overlap creates the weatherproof lap, and the packaging is calibrated to that lap.

The rule has been stable for decades for standard weights, but it is not a universal constant.

Bundles needed = Roof squares × Bundles per square × (1 + waste factor) Where: Roof squares = Roof surface area (sq ft) / 100 Waste factor = 0.10 for a simple gable, 0.15 for a hip or cut-up roof

When a square takes 4 bundles

Heavier shingles need more material for the same 100 sq ft, so manufacturers switch to four bundles per square and print that on the wrapper.

Shingle typeBundles per squareTypical bundle weightCoverage per bundle
3-tab asphalt355–70 lb33.3 sq ft
Architectural / dimensional365–80 lb33.3 sq ft
Premium / designer475–95 lb25 sq ft
Impact-rated (Class 4)3 or 470–90 lb25–33.3 sq ft

The wrapper always wins over any rule of thumb. If the label says four bundles per square, order on four. Insist on seeing the label or the product data sheet before the material is delivered.

Worked example: a 20-square roof

  1. Roof surface: 1,700 sq ft footprint × 1.12 pitch multiplier = 1,904 sq ft
  2. Convert to squares: 1,904 / 100 = 19.04 squares
  3. Round up for ordering: 20 squares
  4. Bundles at the 3-bundle rule: 20 × 3 = 60 bundles
  5. Add 10% waste: 60 × 1.10 = 66 bundles

Sixty-six bundles of architectural shingles weighs roughly 4,300–5,300 lb. That is a full pallet load, so plan for delivery rather than hauling it home in a pickup.

What the bundle count does not cover

Bundles only buy the field shingles. These items are ordered separately and are the most common omission on a homeowner’s material list:

  • Starter strip: one bundle typically covers about 100 linear feet of eave. A simple gable usually needs 1–2 bundles.
  • Ridge cap: hip and ridge cap shingles are a separate SKU, generally 25–35 linear feet per bundle. A gable roof needs 1–2 bundles; a hip roof can need 4 or more.
  • Underlayment: sold by the roll and never by the bundle. Synthetic underlayment covers roughly 10 squares per roll; 15 lb felt covers about 4 squares per roll.
  • Ice & water shield and drip edge: priced by the roll and by the linear foot, and required along eaves and valleys in cold-climate jurisdictions.

Common mistakes with bundle counts

  • Using footprint instead of roof surface. This is the single most common error, and it under-orders by 10–40% depending on pitch.
  • Skipping the waste factor. Hips, valleys, dormers, and chimney cuts all generate offcuts. Ten percent is the floor for a simple gable; fifteen percent is much safer on a cut-up roof.
  • Forgetting starter and ridge. A crew that arrives without ridge cap cannot finish the roof.
  • Assuming 3 bundles when the label says 4. Premium designer lines are the usual trap, and it is a 33% shortfall on the field material.

Age matters as much as quantity

If you are planning a replacement rather than a repair, the bundle count is only half the decision. The other half is how long the new shingles will actually last in your climate — our guide to asphalt shingle lifespan by brand covers the realistic numbers behind the warranty language.

Concrete Bag vs Ready Mix Cost Calculator

Published July 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Deciding between bagged concrete and ready-mix comes down to one number: how many cubic yards you need. This guide shows the break-even point (about one cubic yard) and gives a simple cost calculator you can apply to any project.

Quick answer: The break-even is about 1 cubic yard. Below that, bagged concrete is usually cheaper once you factor delivery minimums. Above it, ready-mix wins every time. Use our Concrete Slab Calculator to size your pour first.

The break-even rule

One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. An 80-pound bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet, so a yard needs roughly 45 bags; a 60-pound bag yields about 0.45 cubic feet, so a yard needs about 60 bags. Multiply the bag count by $4–$6 and compare it with ready-mix at $110–$165 per yard delivered.

1 cubic yard = 27 cu ft 80lb bags per yard = 27 / 0.6 = 45 bags 60lb bags per yard = 27 / 0.45 = 60 bags Bag material cost = bags x $4 to $6 Ready-mix cost = yards x $110 to $165

At exactly one cubic yard, bagged material alone already costs more than delivered ready-mix, and that gap widens fast. The only reason to bag a larger job is site access — when a truck simply cannot reach the pour.

Hidden costs on both sides

  • Ready-mix: Delivery minimums (often 1–2 yards), short-load fees under the minimum, pump-truck rental, and a hard time window.
  • Bags: Mixer rental or a lot of manual labor, plus the time cost of mixing dozens of bags before the concrete sets.
  • Waste: Both methods need a 5–10% buffer; order ready-mix in whole-yard increments.

Worked example: a 12x12 patio at 4 inches

A 12x12 foot patio is 144 square feet. At 4 inches thick that is 144 x 0.333 = 48 cubic feet, or 1.78 cubic yards with 10% waste. Bagged, that is about 82 80lb bags at $4–$6 each — roughly $328–$492 in material. Ready-mix at $110–$165 per yard is about $196–$294 delivered. Even with a delivery minimum, ready-mix wins clearly once you clear one cubic yard.

Regional and seasonal notes

Ready-mix prices climb with fuel costs, aggregate shortages, and peak building season (late spring through summer). In remote or high-demand markets, the per-yard price can run higher than the ranges here, which makes the bagged option relatively more attractive for very small jobs. Treat the numbers as planning estimates and confirm with a local plant.

When the minimum load still bites

Many ready-mix plants charge a short-load fee when you order under their minimum, often one or two cubic yards. If your pour is only 0.6 yards, you may be billed for a full yard anyway, which narrows the gap with bags. Ask the plant about its minimum and short-load policy before deciding; for a true micro-pour under half a yard, bags remain the practical choice despite the higher per-yard rate. The break-even rule is a guide, not a law — site access and plant policies decide the final call.

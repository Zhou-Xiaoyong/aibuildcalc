How Many Square Feet in a Box of Laminate

Published November 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 400 sq ft open living and dining area takes 18 cartons of 8 mm laminate. Divide the same floor by the 25 square feet on the shelf tag and you get 16, which is two cartons short and one more trip to the store.

Quick answer: A box of laminate covers 20 to 25 square feet, and the exact figure is printed on the carton end. 8 mm plank at 7.5 by 47.25 in, ten planks to the carton, works out to 24.6 sq ft. Add 5% for cuts, divide by that coverage, round up. The Laminate Calculator runs the whole order from room dimensions.

The figure on the box is a multiplication

Coverage comes from three numbers: width, length, and how many planks the mill stacked in the carton. Nothing about the room enters into it.

Sq ft per carton = (plank width in x plank length in x planks per carton) / 144 Cartons = (floor sq ft x waste factor) / sq ft per carton, rounded up Worked example, 400 sq ft living and dining, straight lay: 7.5 x 47.25 x 10 = 3,543.75 sq in 3,543.75 / 144 = 24.6 sq ft per carton 400 x 1.05 = 420 sq ft with cuts 420 / 24.6 = 17.1 Order 18 cartons, 442.8 sq ft of material

Thickness moves the count. A 12 mm board at 5 in wide ships twelve to the carton and still lands under 20 sq ft, because thickness eats the stack height before width does.

Two cartons of the same nominal size from different mills can differ by four square feet, a whole carton on a 400 sq ft job.

The 400 sq ft order

Eighteen cartons at 44 lb is 792 lb, one pallet or two trips in a half-ton pickup. Material at $2.40 a sq ft runs $1,063 for the 442.8 sq ft ordered. Install at $2.75 adds $1,100 on the floor, and foam underlayment at $0.45 adds $180. The job lands at $2,343, or $5.86 a sq ft.

Where the count moves

Waste is what a saw turns into offcuts at walls and doorways. It tracks the layout, not the square footage.

  • 5% for an open rectangle with square walls and one doorway.
  • 10% for a hallway, an angled wall, an island, or any room where every course ends in a rip cut.
  • 15% for a diagonal lay, where both ends of every plank are cut.

The same 400 sq ft room on the diagonal needs 460 sq ft of material, which is 19 cartons. Skip that allowance and the last two rows come up short by a plank width.

Production codes matter as much as quantity. Two cartons from different runs read a shade apart along a window wall, so buy the job in one purchase and check every code before the installer opens the first carton.

The count is the easy half. A tile box needs thinset under it; a laminate carton needs foam. Carpet is not sold in cartons, which is why the Carpet Calculator works in square yards.

What else is printed on the carton end

The AC rating sits beside the coverage figure. AC3 covers a normal home, AC4 covers light commercial, and a NALFA mark means the carton was tested to the North American standard. Thickness runs 8 mm on builder grade, 10 mm mid, 12 mm premium. Some cartons carry a 2 mm pad bonded to the back; the rest need foam at $0.30 to $0.60 a sq ft, plus 6 mil poly over a slab.

Two specs decide whether the floor survives year three. Acclimation is 48 hours, cartons flat and unopened in the room. Flatness is 3/16 in over any 10 ft run, tighter than the 1/4 in tile allows, and a hump that size flexes the locking joint on every step. If the floor rocks, check the framing first. The Floor Joist Calculator settles the span.

Leave a 1/4 to 3/8 in gap at every wall and cover it with base and shoe. A floor pinned tight on all four sides buckles in the first dry week of winter.

What to order

Take the carton figure, add 5% in a plain rectangle and 10% where a hallway or angled wall forces rip cuts, divide, round up. Buy one carton beyond that as attic stock, about $59 on a 24.6 sq ft box.

The trade-off is small and real: one carton in the garage for a decade. Against it, a repair two winters out cannot be matched to the original production run, and one damaged plank mid-room becomes a $59 fix instead of a new floor.

Do not substitute a different thickness on that spare, and do not let a retailer split a pallet across two runs. Every estimator in the flooring collection prices a job from floor area, waste factor and price per square foot.

Hardwood Flooring Cost per Square Foot Installed

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Installed hardwood prices are usually quoted as a single per-square-foot number, which makes bids easy to compare and easy to misread. That number bundles a material cost that varies threefold by species with a labour cost that depends on how the floor is fastened, what is underneath it and how many doorways the installer has to work around. Two quotes of $11 per square foot can describe very different floors.

Quick answer: Expect $8 to $18 per square foot installed across the solid and engineered hardwood range in 2026, with most mid-grade oak projects landing at $10 to $13. A 1,000 sq ft job therefore runs about $8,000 to $18,000 before removal, stairs and trim. Price your own square footage with the Hardwood Flooring Calculator.

Material cost by species

Species drives the material line more than any other choice. Domestic oak and maple are the volume products and the most stable in price; hickory costs more because it is harder to mill and to finish consistently; walnut is priced as a premium product and is usually specified for a feature room rather than a whole house. Engineered boards cost less per square foot than solid in the same species because they use a thinner wear layer over a plywood core.

Installation labour by method

Nail-down over a wood subfloor is the standard residential method and the cheapest to execute. Glue-down is used over concrete and costs more in adhesive and time. Floating installations are the fastest but require a flat substrate and an underlayment, and the material usually carries a higher price per square foot. Removal and disposal of the old floor is almost always a separate line, and so is subfloor repair once the old surface comes up.

Solid versus engineered, in cost terms

Engineered hardwood is not a downgrade; it is a different product with a plywood core that tolerates humidity swings and can be installed below grade. Its wear layer determines how many times it can be refinished, typically one to three times against five or more for solid. Over a concrete slab, engineered glued down is often the only practical option, and the price difference versus solid narrows once the cost of adding a plywood subfloor is counted.

What a per-square-foot quote usually leaves out

Stair work is priced per step rather than per square foot, commonly $50 to $100 per tread including the nosing. Baseboard removal and reinstallation runs $2 to $4 per linear foot, and new baseboard costs more. Transitions and reducer strips are $25 to $60 each at every doorway and every change of flooring. Furniture moving, appliance disconnection, sales tax and a minimum job charge are all normal exclusions. On a 1,000 sq ft job these add-ons commonly add $800 to $2,500.

Material = area x material price per sq ft x 1.05 to 1.10 waste Installation = area x labour rate per sq ft Total = material + installation + add-ons (stairs, trim, transitions, removal) Example, 1,000 sq ft red oak at $5.50 material and $4.50 labour: 1,000 x 5.50 x 1.07 = $5,885 1,000 x 4.50 = $4,500 add-ons $1,200 -> about $11,585, or $11.59 per sq ft

How to compare hardwood bids

Ask each contractor to break the bid into material, labour, removal, subfloor work and trim, and to state the species, grade, board width and finish system. A bid that quotes "oak" without a grade is not a bid you can compare. Confirm whether the price includes moving furniture, disposing of the old floor and reinstalling baseboard. Then check the total against the square footage you measured, not the square footage on the quote, because a low per-square-foot rate applied to an inflated area is the oldest trick in flooring sales.

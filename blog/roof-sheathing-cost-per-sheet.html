Roof Sheathing Cost Per Sheet: OSB vs Plywood in 2026

Published September 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Roof sheathing is the layer of structural panels fastened to the roof trusses or rafters that the underlayment and shingles attach to. Most US homes use either OSB or plywood sheathing, and the price gap between them — and the choice of thickness — has a meaningful impact on reroofing budgets. This guide covers per-sheet prices, sheet sizes, thickness rules of thumb, and what to expect for a typical 17-square roof in 2026.

Quick answer: 7/16" OSB roof sheathing runs about $25–$38 per 4×8 sheet in 2026, while 1/2" plywood runs $35–$55 per sheet. A typical 17-square re-roof needs roughly 70–85 sheets, putting the sheathing line item at $1,800–$4,500 before labor. Use our Roof Sheathing Calculator for an exact count from your roof footprint.

Sheet size and thickness basics

Roof sheathing panels in residential construction are almost always 4 feet × 8 feet (= 32 sq ft per sheet). The two standard thicknesses are:

  • 7/16" OSB — the standard for most US roof applications with 16" or 24" rafter spacing.
  • 1/2" plywood or OSB — required in some high-wind zones, snow-load regions, or where the local building code calls for it.
  • 5/8" or 3/4" — uncommon on residential roofs, mostly used where cathedral ceilings or heavy snow loads demand extra rigidity.

The International Residential Code (IRC) sets minimum roof sheathing thickness by span and load; in most jurisdictions 7/16" OSB is the default that passes code for typical truss spacing.

OSB vs plywood at a glance

OSB (oriented strand board) is an engineered panel made from compressed wood strands arranged in layers; plywood is made from cross-laminated wood veneers. Both pass code for roof sheathing when installed correctly, but they differ in cost, weight, moisture behavior, and fastener holding.

For a typical re-roof where the existing layer is torn off and replaced, OSB is the budget default and what most contractors will quote. Plywood is the upgrade when the homeowner wants a stiffer panel, faster dry-out if a future leak occurs, or is building in a region that calls for it.

How many sheets does a 17-square roof need?

The quick rule: 1 sheet of 4×8 sheathing covers 32 sq ft, so a 1,700 sq ft roof footprint (the median US home) at a 6/12 pitch (~17 squares of actual roof surface) requires about 75 sheets. Add 5–10% for cuts, waste, and damaged panels during install:

Sheets needed (raw) = Roof area (sq ft) / 32 Sheets to order = raw count × 1.08 (8% waste factor)

For a 17-square roof (~1,700 sq ft of actual surface area):

  • Raw sheets: 1,700 / 32 = 53 sheets
  • With 8% waste: 53 × 1.08 = ~58 sheets
  • If you only replace damaged panels (a partial re-sheet): typical range is 10–25 sheets.

Plywood grades explained

Most residential roof sheathing uses CDX plywood: C and D are the two face grades (C being smoother), and X means “exterior glue.” It is structural, code-approved, and the lowest-cost exterior-grade plywood. For visible roof applications (exposed beams, tongue-and-groove ceilings), upgrade to BC or AC for a smoother face.

Other plywood designations you may see at the lumberyard:

  • OSB is the dominant alternative; it is engineered for structural use and costs less per sheet.
  • ZIP System or DensDeck panels are sheathing with a built-in weather-resistant overlay; they replace the separate felt underlayment at the deck level and add $8–$15 per sheet.
  • Radiant barrier sheathing adds a foil face that reflects radiant heat; common in hot climates for attic cooling, adds $5–$12 per sheet.

When to replace sheathing during a re-roof

Most re-roofs leave the existing sheathing in place if it is dry, firm, and free of rot. Replacement is triggered when:

  • The contractor finds soft spots when walking the deck.
  • Roof leaks have saturated the panels over time.
  • The home is being re-roofed for the third time with layers built up; building code usually caps at two layers and may force a tear-off to deck.
  • The original sheathing is 1×6 board decking and the new roofing system calls for a continuous plywood/OSB deck.

Budget a contingency of $1,000–$3,000 on a re-roofing project for partial sheathing replacement; the actual number depends on what the contractor finds after tear-off.

Tips for keeping the cost down

  • Order in full lifts. Lumberyards deliver OSB in lifts of ~70 sheets at a small per-lift discount; partial orders pay a premium.
  • Stage delivery the day of install. OSB stored on-site in rain will absorb moisture and swell, leading to wasted panels.
  • Stick to 7/16" unless your code requires more. Stepping up to 1/2" adds 15–25% to the per-sheet cost without a meaningful performance gain on typical truss spacing.
  • Ask the contractor to identify damaged panels only. A partial re-sheet is often enough, and saves thousands.

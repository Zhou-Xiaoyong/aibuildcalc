Thanksgiving Guests in the Den: How Many Smoke and CO Alarms? 8 Devices

Published November 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 2,400 sq ft house with four bedrooms and a den holding two air mattresses needs eight alarm devices: five smoke alarms in the sleeping rooms, two combination smoke and CO units in the halls outside them, one more combo in the basement. The parts cost $334 and the work takes 90 minutes with a step stool.

Quick answer: count sleeping rooms, add one alarm outside each separate sleeping area, then one on every story including the basement. For a four-bedroom house with a den in use that is eight devices, three of them combo smoke and carbon monoxide: five smoke-only units at $32 plus three combos at $58, or $334. If the ceiling is already hardwired, do not swap in $20 battery units, because that breaks the interconnection that wakes the house. The Egress Window Calculator prices the other half of a sleeping room.

The den becomes a sleeping room at 6 p.m. Wednesday

The code defines a sleeping room by use, not by the tax card. Once a mattress sits in a room with a door and a window, that room is where the alarm has to be, on the sleeping side of the door. A closed door buys 10 to 15 minutes of escape time in a house fire, and an alarm in the hall on the other side of it buys nothing.

Thanksgiving adds sleeping rooms faster than any other week: the den, the sectional in the basement rec room, a pack-and-play in the office. Be honest about what is fixable tonight. A den with a 44-inch window sill is not a legal bedroom, and the egress window requirements run $4,000 to $7,000 installed once a foundation cut is involved. You are not doing that this week. The alarm takes 20 minutes.

Where the eight devices go

IRC R314 asks for an alarm in each sleeping room, one outside each separate sleeping area in the immediate vicinity of the bedrooms, and one on each additional story. R315 adds CO coverage outside the sleeping areas and on any story with a fuel-burning appliance.

The basement unit is the one people skip, and it carries the most risk here, because that is where the furnace, the water heater and the visiting cousin all end up. Put the CO alarm outside the basement sleeping area, never taped to the furnace, where it reads flue gas before the gas has mixed with room air and tells you nothing. An attached garage needs coverage too, even in an all-electric house, since a car left running while somebody loads a suitcase is the usual source. If a heater is going into that garage, the garage BTU arithmetic sizes it.

Five photoelectric units and three combos at mid-range pricing is the $334 figure. Skip the $15 ionization units for any room near the kitchen, because a 20 lb bird going into a 425°F oven and a door opened every 20 minutes to baste puts a plume into the hall that an ionization chamber reads as smoke. An alarm that screams at 4 p.m. is an alarm somebody silences with a towel for the weekend. One rule varies by state: California and New York, among others, require a 10-year sealed battery on replacement, so the $15 model is not legal to install there.

The 48-hour version, in order

One trip, eight devices and a step stool, and no second run to the store on Wednesday.

  • Tonight. Pull every alarm off the ceiling and read the date stamped on the back. Past 10 years it is dead even if the test button chirps, because the sensor ages and the button only tests the sounder.
  • Tonight. If only three units fit the budget, put them in the basement, the den and the upstairs hall. Those three cover every sleeping person on Thursday.
  • Tomorrow morning. Install the sleeping-room units at the center of each ceiling, 3 ft from a bathroom door and 10 ft from the oven.
  • Tomorrow afternoon. Test each unit with someone standing in the farthest bedroom with the door shut. If they cannot hear it, the location or the interconnection is wrong.
  • Wednesday. Nothing. You are not on a ladder while cars pull in.

The heater is the other Wednesday problem. A 1,500 W space heater pulls 12.5 A, and a 15 A bedroom circuit is good for 12 A of continuous load, so the heater plus a phone charger is already past the line. The Circuit Load Calculator runs that per circuit, and the Thanksgiving amp draw breakdown covers the kitchen side. Wall outlet on a 20 A circuit, never a power strip, never an extension cord under a rug.

What not to do

Do not pull the hall alarm because it went off during prep. Move it 3 ft from the kitchen door or swap the ionization unit for a photoelectric one. An alarm in a drawer protects nobody.

Do not trust the unit a previous owner installed in 2009, and do not set a battery CO alarm on top of the water heater. Both read as coverage on paper and as nothing on the night it matters.

Thanksgiving alarm questions

Can I use battery alarms if the house already has hardwired ones?

Only if they interconnect, which means the $45 to $70 wireless models. Code requires every alarm to sound together, and a standalone unit in the den leaves the den out of the network. Adding two hardwired locations runs $250 to $450 for the first and $90 to $150 after that.

How many for a house with no basement?

Seven: five sleeping rooms and two halls, since the story rule was only adding the one device. CO drops to the two hall combos unless the furnace and water heater sit in a main-floor closet, in which case a third unit goes outside that door.

Do I need CO alarms with no gas appliances?

Not under R315 if the house is all-electric with no attached garage and no wood fireplace. The garage is the exception that matters on a holiday, since an idling car is the most common CO source in a house that burns nothing.

The call

Buy eight devices, three of them combo, and hang them tonight. That is $334 and 90 minutes, less than the turkey, and it covers every person sleeping under the roof. Sleeping rooms first, halls second, basement third.

The trade-off is honest: you are spending $334 on hardware you hope never sounds, and the photoelectric units will still trip once on Thursday when the oven door opens. The alternative is five people asleep behind closed doors with the only alarm 30 ft away on the far side of one. If the den stays a bedroom past January, the guest bedroom conversion cost puts the egress window and closet at $1,180 for a spare room and $16,400 for a basement build, and that one needs a permit.

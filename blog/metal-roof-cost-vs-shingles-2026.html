Metal Roof Cost vs Shingles 2026

Published August 2026 · 9 min read · By AIBuildCalc

Standing seam metal costs two to three times an architectural shingle roof on installation day, which is why sticker shock ends a lot of conversations. The more useful question is what each roof costs per year of service, and what you actually spend over 25 years once re-roofing, energy and insurance are counted. This comparison uses 2026 US installed price ranges and a consistent 22-square roof.

Quick answer: In 2026, architectural shingles install for roughly $450 to $750 per square and last 25 to 30 years; standing seam metal runs $900 to $1,600 per square and lasts 40 to 70. Annualized, they land within a few dollars of each other per square. Price your own roof with the Metal Roof Calculator.

2026 installed cost per square

Per-square pricing is the only fair way to compare, because it already folds in underlayment, flashing, labor and disposal. These are installed ranges for a typical single-family roof in 2026; regional labor rates move them by 20% or more in either direction.

The right-hand column is the surprise. Once you divide by expected service life, the annual carrying cost of the cheapest and the most expensive roof on the list is broadly similar. The real difference is timing: asphalt asks for small amounts repeatedly, metal asks for one large amount once.

Read as pure cash outlay over 25 years, architectural asphalt wins by a wide margin. Read as value remaining at year 25, standing seam is the only option on the list with roughly half its service life left, which is the argument for metal if you expect to stay in the house decades rather than years.

What narrows the gap

Three factors typically work in metal's favor. First, energy: a reflective or cool-pigment metal roof can cut summer cooling costs by roughly 7% to 15% in hot-summer climates, which for many houses is a typical $75 to $250 a year, or $1,900 to $6,250 over 25 years. Results depend heavily on attic insulation and duct sealing, so treat the low end as the realistic case in mixed climates.

Second, insurance: metal roofing frequently carries a Class 4 impact rating and higher wind ratings, and in hail- or hurricane-prone states carriers often apply credits commonly ranging from 5% to 35% off the wind and hail portion of the premium. The discount varies enormously by state and carrier, so call your insurer before counting it.

Third, tear-off savings: metal weighs roughly 0.7 to 1.5 lb per square foot against asphalt's 2 to 4 lb, so it can often go over one existing shingle layer where code allows. That cuts job cost, though you never get to inspect the deck.

Maintenance and hidden costs on both sides

Exposed-fastener metal has a specific weakness: the rubber gaskets under the screws degrade, so most roofs need a fastener replacement cycle around year 10 to 20. Standing seam hides its fasteners, which is part of why it costs more. Asphalt's weakness is granule loss and thermal cracking late in life, plus storm damage that usually shows up as repairs.

Either way, the deck is what people forget to budget for. Soft, delaminated, or under-thickness sheathing is a separate line item — size it with the Roof Sheathing Calculator. Pitch also drives labor and safety setup on metal especially, so confirm it with the Roof Pitch Calculator before requesting bids.

When shingles are still the right call

Choose asphalt if you plan to sell within roughly a decade, if the budget simply does not stretch, if an HOA covenant restricts the visible material, or if the roof is complex enough that standing seam panel fabrication waste would be severe. Choose metal if you are staying long term, live somewhere with hail or wildfire exposure, or want to avoid ever paying for a second tear-off. If you are in between, corrugated or stamped metal shingle sits in the middle on both price and lifespan, and is the option most often overlooked.

Laminate vs Hardwood Flooring Cost

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 1,000 sq ft living area in solid red oak comes back at roughly $11,900 installed. The same area in 12 mm laminate lands near $6,700. That $5,200 gap buys a floor that cannot be sanded, and whether it is worth closing comes down to what is under the floor and how long you plan to stay.

Quick answer: Laminate runs $3 to $8 per square foot installed in 2026. Hardwood runs $8 to $18. Laminate is the better buy over a slab, below grade, and in a house you will sell inside ten years. Solid hardwood wins over a wood subfloor in a house you intend to keep. Size the laminate half with the Laminate Floor Calculator.

Installed cost, side by side

Material is the line most shoppers compare and the one that matters least. On a 1,000 sq ft job, labor and prep outweigh the gap between a $2.80 plank and a $4.20 plank.

Those are national bands. Crews in the Northeast, coastal California and the Seattle metro price 25 to 40 percent above the Midwest, and small jobs carry a minimum charge that doubles the per-square-foot rate on paper.

Why the products price apart

Laminate is a printed image under a clear wear layer, bonded to an HDF core. There is no wood on the surface, so it cannot be sanded. Wear layers are rated AC1 through AC5, and AC4 is the grade worth buying.

Solid hardwood is a 3/4 inch board of one species, sandable four to six times across a 60 to 100 year life. Engineered sits between them: a 2 to 6 mm wear layer over plywood, refinishable once to three times.

The carton math, with the waste that actually applies

Both floors ship in cartons, but waste differs. Laminate floats with a 1/4 to 3/8 inch gap at every wall, and the last row is nearly always a rip cut, so 10 percent is the working allowance; a room with more than four corners needs 15. Hardwood is ordered at 5 to 7 percent because offcuts get used.

Laminate cartons = (room sq ft x 1.10) / sq ft per carton Hardwood board = room sq ft x 1.05 to 1.07 (use 1.10 for diagonal or herringbone) Underlayment = room sq ft, sold in 100 sq ft rolls Worked example, 1,000 sq ft, 12 mm laminate at 20.1 sq ft per carton: 1,000 x 1.10 = 1,100 sq ft 1,100 / 20.1 = 54.7 -> order 55 cartons Same room in 3/4 in oak: 1,000 x 1.07 = 1,070 sq ft of board

Cost over twenty years

The installed price is half the comparison. The other half is year twelve, when laminate shows dull traffic lanes and hardwood is ready for a screen and recoat.

The laminate figure assumes one replacement. In a bedroom with light traffic and no pets, a quality AC4 floor often runs 15 to 20 years untouched and the totals converge. In a kitchen with a dog, they do not.

Where laminate wins outright

Below grade. Laminate tolerates a concrete slab and basement humidity swings better than solid oak, which cups and gaps once indoor relative humidity drifts outside 35 to 55 percent. Rentals and short holds, where you will never capture the value of a refinish.

Where hardwood wins outright

Any house you will own past ten years with a wood subfloor already in place. Kitchens and entries, where a laminate seam that takes on water swells and cannot be repaired, only replaced. Higher-end resale, where an appraiser treats solid wood as an upgrade and laminate as neutral.

The constraints that move a bid

Flatness is the one homeowners miss. A floating floor needs the substrate within 3/16 inch over 10 feet, and a slab that fails needs self-leveler at $1 to $3 per sq ft. Solid hardwood needs a 3/4 inch subfloor over joists that deflect no more than L/360 under live load; a bouncy floor is a structural repair in a different budget line. Upper-floor condos usually require an acoustic underlayment rated to IIC 50, which adds $0.50 to $1.50 per sq ft and rarely appears in a laminate quote.

Door clearance is the second. Three-quarters of an inch of hardwood plus underlayment can trap a door, and undercutting runs $25 to $50 each. The Floor Joist Calculator checks span and deflection when the subfloor is the open question.

How to compare the two bids

Get the same five lines from both: material with product name and thickness, underlayment or fasteners, labor, removal of the old floor, and trim plus transitions. A laminate bid with no underlayment is $0.40 to $1.00 per sq ft light; a hardwood bid with no removal is $1.00 to $2.00 light.

If this is a house you are keeping and the subfloor is wood, buy the hardwood and stop shopping. If it is a basement, a rental or a five-year house, a 12 mm AC4 laminate over quality underlayment is the better spend, and the Carpet Calculator will show whether carpet is cheaper for the bedrooms. The trade-off with laminate is simple: the day it fails, it fails as a whole floor.

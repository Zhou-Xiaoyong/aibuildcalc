Linoleum Flooring Cost per Square Foot

Published November 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 12 by 16 ft kitchen prices at $1,344 in entry sheet linoleum and $2,172 in 2.5 mm Marmoleum. Both bids buy 24 square yards for a floor that measures 21.3, and that rounding is the first thing to question on a sheet-goods quote.

Quick answer: Linoleum costs $2.50 to $7.50 per square foot in material and $4.50 to $11.50 installed. Entry sheet at 2.0 mm runs near $3, 2.5 mm Marmoleum near $5.50, and 13 in composition tile $3.50 to $5.50. Dealers quote the square yard, so multiply a yard price by 9 before you compare it to a square-foot bid. The Linoleum Calculator turns room dimensions into square yards and a roll cut length.

Square yards, square feet, and a factor of nine

Resilient sheet goods are sold by the square yard in the US. That unit appears nowhere else in a renovation, and it hides the price.

Twenty-six dollars a yard is $2.89 a square foot. Fifty dollars a yard is $5.56. Line either figure up against $6 a square foot for luxury vinyl plank and the sheet looks half the price. It usually is cheaper, though not by the margin those two numbers suggest.

Sq yd = (length ft x width ft) / 9 Drops = room width / roll width, rounded up Linear ft = drops x room length Order sq yd = (linear ft x roll width) / 9 Worked example, 12 x 16 ft kitchen, 6 ft 7 in roll: 12 / 6.58 = 1.8, so 2 drops 2 x 16 = 32 linear ft 32 x 6.58 = 210.6 sq ft 210.6 / 9 = 23.4 sq yd, order 24 24 x $26 = $624, entry sheet 24 x $50 = $1,200, 2.5 mm Marmoleum

Waste on a sheet floor follows drop count, not a percentage tacked on at the end. A 12 ft room and a 15 ft roll take one drop. The same room on a 6 ft 7 in roll takes two, and the second drop is mostly offcut.

Labor is not where these bids diverge

Bid A carries $480 of labor against a $1,344 total, or 36%. Bid B carries $672 against $2,172, or 31%. The $828 gap between the jobs is material and adhesive almost to the dollar, which means the sheet grade is the decision and the installer is the same person either way.

Nobody is being overcharged for laying 192 sq ft of goods.

Four specs that decide year three

Flatness first. Most sheet-goods specs call for 3/16 in over any 10 ft run, checked with a straightedge and a shim, and 2.5 mm lino shows a 1/8 in dip as a shadow line under a window. Sanding the seams and feather patching runs $0.90 to $1.50 a square foot on the area that needs it.

Moisture second. A slab wants ASTM F1869 calcium chloride at 3 lb per 1,000 sq ft per 24 hours or less, or F2170 probes under 75% relative humidity. Jute backing wicks water, so sheet linoleum over a slab on grade needs a vapor retarder underneath and a reading before the roll is opened.

Acclimation third. Rolls stand upright in the room at 65 to 85 F for 48 hours. Cold material will not flatten at the seam, and a curled edge will not glue back down.

Deflection last. A bouncy floor cracks the seam before it cracks anything else, and L/360 is the working limit for resilient goods. The Floor Joist Calculator settles the span before anyone orders material.

What to buy

Buy 2.5 mm Marmoleum sheet at $45 to $63 a yard for a kitchen, mudroom, or hallway that has to last thirty years. The pattern runs through the full thickness, so a scuff never shows a white core the way printed vinyl does, and a dull floor can be refinished instead of replaced.

Buy 2.0 mm entry sheet in a rental unit or a room already scheduled for replacement. It saves $576 on the same 24 yards and gives up about a decade of service life.

The trade-off is the seam. A 6 ft 7 in roll cannot cross a 12 ft room in one piece, so the joint has to land somewhere sensible and get heat welded, and a bad weld is permanent. Expect 48 hours of cure before furniture goes back, and skip this floor entirely on a slab that reads high.

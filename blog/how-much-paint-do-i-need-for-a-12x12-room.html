How Much Paint Do I Need for a 12x12 Room?

Published September 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 12 by 12 room with an 8 foot ceiling is the most common bedroom size in the United States, which makes it a useful unit of measure. The answer is short: about two gallons for the walls in two coats, or three gallons if the ceiling is painted too. The useful part is knowing why.

Quick answer: A 12x12 room with 8 ft ceilings has about 384 square feet of wall surface, or roughly 333 square feet after subtracting one door and two windows. Two coats at 350 to 400 square feet per gallon comes to about 1.9 gallons, so buy 2 gallons for the walls. Paint the ceiling as well and the total paintable area rises to about 477 square feet, which rounds to 3 gallons. Use our Paint Calculator for a room of any size.

The number that actually matters: paintable area

Paint is sold by volume and consumed by area, so the first step is converting the room into square feet of surface.

Perimeter (ft) = 2 x (Length + Width) Wall area (sq ft) = Perimeter x Ceiling height - Openings Ceiling area (sq ft) = Length x Width Gallons per coat = Area / Coverage (sq ft per gallon) Total gallons = Gallons per coat x Coats, then round up to whole containers

For a 12x12x8 room the perimeter is 2 x (12 + 12), or 48 feet. Multiply by 8 feet of height and the gross wall area is 384 square feet. Subtract a standard 36 by 84 inch door at about 21 square feet and two 15 square foot windows, and roughly 51 square feet come off, leaving 333 square feet of wall. The ceiling adds 144 square feet. Walls only, work with 333; walls and ceiling, 477.

How much one gallon really covers

The coverage printed on the can is a best case.

  • 350 to 400 square feet on a smooth, previously painted or properly primed wall. This is the number most interior paints are rated at.
  • 250 to 300 square feet on bare new drywall, which drinks the first coat.
  • 300 to 350 square feet on lightly textured or orange-peel walls.
  • 200 to 250 square feet on heavy texture, masonry, or bare wood.

Two coats do not cost exactly twice one coat. The second coat covers better because the surface is already sealed, improving coverage by roughly 10 to 15 percent. A practical working figure for two coats on previously painted interior walls is about 350 square feet of wall per gallon.

Primer is a separate purchase

Primer and paint are different products, and a 12x12 room usually needs one to two gallons of primer if the walls are new or freshly patched. Bare drywall demands primer: joint compound and paper facing absorb unevenly, and skipping it produces a blotchy finish no matter how many topcoats follow. General-purpose drywall primer covers roughly 400 square feet per gallon, so a 12x12 room takes one gallon, or two with the ceiling. Previously painted walls in sound condition generally do not need primer unless you are making a large color change, in which case a tinted primer reduces the topcoats required. Spot-priming patches is always worth the ten minutes.

The math versus the shelf

Interior wall paint is sold in quarts, one-gallon cans, and five-gallon pails. A quart covers roughly 100 square feet, so a 12x12 room sits squarely in one-gallon territory. Buy all cans from the same batch or lot number, because tint can vary between production runs and the difference is visible on a large flat wall. Keep about a quart of each color for touch-ups, seal the can, store it upside down, and keep it from freezing. One extra can is cheaper than a mid-wall trip, and unopened stock colors can usually be returned.

Ceilings and trim add a little more

Ceiling paint is formulated to spatter less and hide better at a flat finish, and 144 square feet of ceiling takes about three quarters of a gallon for two coats. Trim in a 12x12 room is roughly 48 feet of baseboard plus door and window casing, about 20 square feet of surface; two coats of trim enamel fit in a single quart, and brushing the door slab as well still fits.

The whole job in one line

Walls only, two coats, same color, previously painted: 333 square feet times two is 666 square feet of film, divided by 350 square feet of coverage per gallon gives 1.9, so buy two gallons. At $25 to $70 per gallon for mid-grade interior paint and $70 to $100 or more for premium lines, that is roughly $50 to $200 in paint, plus $40 to $80 for tape, roller covers, a tray, and drop cloths.

Cost to Pour a Concrete Driveway 2026

Published August 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A poured concrete driveway is one of the higher-ticket exterior projects a homeowner takes on, and pricing varies more by region and site conditions than most people expect. Going into 2026, ready-mix and labor costs have largely stabilized after several volatile years, but the spread between a low-cost southern market and a high-cost coastal metro is still roughly two to one. This guide breaks down what you should expect to pay per square foot, what drives the number up, and what a typical driveway costs end to end.

Quick answer: Most homeowners pay approximately $8 to $18 per square foot for a standard 4- to 6-inch broom-finished concrete driveway installed in 2026, which works out to roughly $4,000 to $8,600 for a typical two-car driveway. Run your own numbers with the Concrete Driveway Calculator.

What the per-square-foot price actually includes

When a contractor quotes a driveway at, say, $12 per square foot, that number normally bundles excavation, a compacted gravel base, forming, reinforcement, the concrete itself, placement, finishing, and control joints. It usually does not include removing an existing driveway, retaining walls, drainage structures, or permits. Ask any bidder to itemize those separately so you are comparing like with like.

Roughly speaking, material is 35-45% of the total and labor is the balance. That ratio matters because it explains why prices track local wage rates far more closely than they track the price of cement.

Regional price ranges

Labor availability, permit overhead, and winter air-entrainment requirements all push regional pricing apart. These are approximate 2026 installed ranges for a standard 4-inch broom-finished driveway:

Total cost by driveway size

Multiply your square footage by the regional rate to get a working budget. Common residential footprints:

Total cost = Length (ft) x Width (ft) x Cost per sq ft Single car (12 x 24 = 288 sq ft) @ $11 = $3,170 Double car (20 x 24 = 480 sq ft) @ $11 = $5,280 Long single (12 x 50 = 600 sq ft) @ $11 = $6,600 Wide double (24 x 40 = 960 sq ft) @ $11 = $10,560

Concrete volume for the same slabs is straightforward: a 480 sq ft driveway at 5 inches thick is 480 x (5/12) / 27 = 7.4 cubic yards before waste, or about 8.1 yards with a 10% allowance.

What pushes the price up

  • Demolition and haul-off: removing an existing slab typically adds $2 - $6 per square foot.
  • Poor soil: soft or organic soil may require over-excavation and imported structural fill.
  • Slope and drainage: anything past a mild grade needs forming labor, and sometimes a trench drain at $50 - $110 per linear foot.
  • Reinforcement: upgrading from wire mesh to a #4 rebar grid adds roughly $0.80 - $2.00 per square foot.
  • Short loads: ready-mix suppliers charge a short-load fee, often $75 - $200, on orders under about 4 - 5 yards.
  • Sealing: optional but recommended in freeze-thaw climates, at about $0.50 - $1.50 per square foot every few years.

Concrete vs. the alternatives

Asphalt usually comes in cheaper up front, around $5 - $10 per square foot in 2026, but needs sealcoating every few years and has a shorter service life. Pavers run $15 - $30 per square foot installed and are the most repairable. Poured concrete sits in the middle on cost and generally lasts 30 years or more with routine joint maintenance, which is why it remains the default choice in most US markets. If your project also involves a retaining edge or a garden wall along the drive, the block calculator will size that portion for you.

Getting an accurate number for your project

Measure your actual driveway footprint rather than relying on a rule of thumb, decide on thickness based on the heaviest vehicle that will park there, and add 10% for waste.

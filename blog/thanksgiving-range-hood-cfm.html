How Many CFM for a Thanksgiving Turkey? 630 CFM, $1,900

Published November 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A four-burner gas range rated around 45,000 BTU plus an 18,000 BTU oven burner needs 630 CFM of exhaust, which is one CFM per 100 BTU of connected input. That number clears turkey smoke and roasting fat for four hours without starving the flame. A 600 CFM ducted hood with new 8-inch duct runs $1,300 to $2,600 installed in November 2026, and the same job crosses the 400 CFM makeup air line that most kitchens never check.

Quick answer: match the hood to the burners, not to the cabinet width. A 30-inch gas range totals 40,000 to 50,000 BTU on the top and adds another 16,000 to 18,000 in the oven, so 600 CFM is the honest target and 900 CFM is what a 36-inch pro-style top needs. Run the numbers with the Airflow Calculator, then confirm the duct can carry it with the Duct Size Calculator before you order anything. Nine days is enough for a wall cap and a hood. It is not enough for a roofer, an electrician, and a mechanical inspector in the same week.

The oven burner is the one people forget

Hood sizing starts from connected input, and the range top is only part of it. A common 30-inch gas range rates its four burners at roughly 9,000, 9,000, 12,000 and 15,000 BTU, which totals 45,000. The oven's bake burner adds 16,000 to 18,000 more, and on the fourth Thursday of November that burner is the one running from noon until four.

The working rule in the trade is 1 CFM of exhaust for every 100 BTU an hour of gas input, so 63,000 BTU comes out at 630 CFM. Hoods are sold in round numbers, which is why the shelf says 600 and the next step up is 900. Buying the 600 and running it on high all afternoon is the normal, defensible choice. Buying a 300 CFM builder-grade hood for a gas range is how a house smells like rendered fat until Sunday.

Electric and induction ranges follow a different rule because there is no combustion product to clear. The convention there is 100 CFM per 10 amps of draw at 240 volts, so a 40-amp range lands at 400 CFM, and the load a hood actually fights is moisture and grease rather than carbon monoxide. If you are choosing between the two this month, that 230 CFM gap is one more reason induction hoods cost less to install.

Capture happens in the first eighteen inches

A hood does not pull smoke out of a room. It catches a plume that widens as it rises, and the plume leaves a 30-inch range at roughly the width of the largest pan. That is why the hood should overhang the cooking surface by 3 inches on each side, and why a 36-inch hood over a 30-inch range outperforms a 30-inch hood with the same blower.

Height matters as much. Gas ranges want the hood 24 to 30 inches above the grates and electric ranges 20 to 24, which is manufacturer territory and worth reading rather than guessing. Every extra inch of height widens the plume before the hood sees it, so the low end of the range captures better and blocks the stockpot. Thanksgiving is a bad week to discover that the hood sits at 32 inches because the cabinet above it was hung for a microwave.

Depth is the third number, and the one most spec sheets bury. A 24-inch-deep hood covers the front burners and the oven door swing. An 18-inch-deep under-cabinet hood leaves the front third of the range uncovered, and that is where the roasting pan sits when you pull it out to baste.

Path two is the value play and it depends entirely on the duct. Path four is not a joke. In a house with a natural-draft water heater and a 600 CFM hood, the hood can outrun the flue and pull combustion gas backward down the vent. More on that below, because it is the one line item that turns a $1,900 job into a $3,400 job.

The 400 CFM line, and what happens past it

Section 505.2 of the International Mechanical Code requires makeup air where a domestic kitchen exhaust hood exceeds 400 CFM, and most states have adopted some version of it. The requirement exists because a hood is a giant fan attached to a sealed box. Pull 600 CFM out of a 1,800-square-foot house and you have asked for 36,000 cubic feet an hour of replacement air, which the house will find through the path of least resistance: a dryer vent, a bath fan flap, or the flue of a naturally drafting water heater.

The failure is quiet and it is real. A water heater with a draft hood needs the house at roughly neutral pressure, and field guidance from the weatherization trade puts the spillage threshold near 5 pascals of depressurization. A 600 CFM hood in a tight house crosses that easily. The test takes ten minutes: close the house up, run the hood on high, and hold a smoke pencil or a match at the draft hood. If the smoke curls out instead of up, the hood is stronger than the flue, and the holiday heating cost breakdown shows what that much replacement air does to a gas bill in January.

The fix is a makeup air path sized for about 80 percent of the exhaust, delivered through a motorized damper interlocked with the hood, a pair of transfer grilles, or a ducted intake with a tempering heater in cold climates. Budget $350 to $900 for the damper and grille version. A heat-recovery unit for a Minnesota January is $1,500 and up, which is why a lot of northern kitchens settle for a 400 CFM hood and a window cracked at the far end of the house, and notice the heating bill in January instead.

Duct, damper, and one 15-amp circuit

Rigid galvanized duct is the only thing that delivers the number on the box. A 600 CFM hood necked down to 4-inch flexible duct at the transition moves closer to 250 CFM, because the velocity climbs past 4,500 feet per minute and the blower stalls against its own static pressure. If the run is short and straight, 7-inch rigid works. If there is one elbow and a wall cap, 8-inch is the spec, and the transition from the hood's 8-inch collar should be a taper, not a step.

Seams get mastic, not tape alone, and the exterior cap needs a backdraft damper that closes. A stuck flap turns the hood into a cold-air intake all winter, and it is the first thing to check when a kitchen feels drafty at the counter.

Electrical is the easy part. A residential hood draws 2 to 4 amps at 120 volts, so the existing 15-amp circuit almost always carries the new unit. Check the load with the Amp Calculator before you add the blower to a circuit that already runs the disposal, and read the Thanksgiving dinner load numbers if the range, the oven, and the warming drawer all land on the same hour.

Two details worth the ten minutes. Order the hood before the duct kit, because the collar size on the unit decides the duct, not the other way around. And pull the old filter the day before, soak it in degreaser, and put it back dry. A mesh filter loaded with last year's fat cuts airflow by a third and drips onto a hot roasting pan at the worst moment.

Thanksgiving range hood questions

Will opening a window fix the smoke?

It helps the smell and does nothing for the grease. An open window relieves the pressure a hood creates, which is genuinely useful if you already have a 600 CFM unit, but a kitchen with no exhaust still coats the cabinet fronts above the range in a film you will find with a rag in February. The window is a supplement, not a substitute.

Does a recirculating hood do anything for a turkey?

It catches some grease on the mesh and pushes the rest of the air back into the room, minus a little odor if the charcoal filter is fresh. For four hours of roasting, that means the moisture and the smoke stay in the kitchen. Recirculating hoods are a reasonable answer for an induction cooktop used for pasta. They are not an answer for a gas oven running all afternoon.

Can a 600 CFM hood backdraft my water heater?

Yes, in a tight house with a naturally drafting flue, and the test is a smoke pencil at the draft hood with the fan on high. If it spills, you need makeup air or you need to run the hood on low until the damper is installed. This is also the conversation to have with the mechanical inspector before you cut the hole, not after.

The call

If the duct above the range is already 6-inch round or 3.25-by-10, buy a 400 CFM ducted hood for $700, install it this weekend, and accept a little smoke when you sear the first batch of sprouts. That is the highest return per dollar in this article, and it finishes before the 26th.

If there is no duct and the range is gas, do not force 600 CFM into a ten-day deadline. A 600 CFM hood without makeup air is a depressurized house and a water heater that spills, and the fix is another $350 to $900 you have not budgeted. Book the wall penetration, the 8-inch run, and the interlocked damper for March, run a $60 box fan in the window this year, and let the kitchen smell like dinner instead of a construction site.

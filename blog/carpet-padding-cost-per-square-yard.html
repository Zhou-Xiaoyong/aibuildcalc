Carpet Padding Cost Per Square Yard

Published October 2026 · 8 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A 1,200 sq ft duplex in Fort Wayne needs carpet on both floors. One bid puts the pad at $588, the other at $1,008, and neither contractor explains the gap. Both numbers are per square yard, a unit nobody quotes carpet in.

Quick answer: Carpet padding runs $2.70 to $18 per square yard in material in 2026. Bonded rebond at 6 lb and 3/8 in, the pad in most homes, sits at $3.60 to $5.40 a yard. Labor is bundled into the carpet install; a pad-only swap adds $2 to $4 a yard. The Carpet Calculator turns room dimensions into yards, rolls and a pad order.

Nine square feet to the yard

Pad is cut from 6 ft rolls and priced by the square yard. Carpet is priced by the square foot. Nine square feet make a yard, so $4.20 of pad is $0.47 a foot next to carpet at $1.80 a foot.

That mismatch is where quotes go wrong. Divide when you should multiply and the pad on 1,200 sq ft reads as $47 instead of $588. Roll width adds waste, since a 12 ft room takes two drops and a 13 ft room three.

Rebond is shredded foam bonded into a slab, and the 8 lb version holds its loft a decade under a sectional. Frothed foam crushes flat in five years.

The 1,200 sq ft order

Pad ships in full rolls: 6 ft by 45 ft, or 30 sq yd.

Pad sq yd = floor sq ft / 9 Order sq yd = pad sq yd x 1.05 Rolls = order sq yd / 30, rounded up Pad cost = order sq yd x price per sq yd Worked example, 1,200 sq ft duplex: 1,200 / 9 = 133.3 sq yd 133.3 x 1.05 = 140 sq yd 140 / 30 = 4.7, so 5 rolls 140 x $4.20 = $588 6 lb rebond 140 x $7.20 = $1,008 8 lb rebond

Full-roll pricing adds 10 yards you pay for and never lay.

Density is the number that matters

A pound rating is density per cubic foot of foam, not the weight of the piece in your hand.

Two pads at 7/16 in, one 6 lb and one 8 lb, feel identical in the showroom and behave differently under a piano. The 6 lb one compresses to the backing and lets the weave grind on the subfloor.

Warranty language is where density turns into money. Most mills tie the wear warranty to cushion carrying the CRI Seal of Approval, capped at 7/16 in for stretch-in carpet and 1/4 in for loop and berber. Half-inch memory foam under a berber voids that warranty, and the loop tips fuzz in two years.

Where pad money goes to waste

  • Direct glue-down. Carpet glued to a slab takes no pad. A pad line on a glue-down basement bid is $600 of nothing.
  • Stairs. Twelve risers at 3 ft wide run about 53 sq ft, and every tread is a separate cut. Budget 15% waste, not 5%.
  • A slab below grade. Rebond is recycled foam and wicks moisture. Rubber or frothed foam over a vapor barrier is the right call, and most mills cap slab readings at 75% to 80% relative humidity on an ASTM F2170 probe.
  • Radiant heat. Thick memory foam insulates the tube and the floor never warms. Use 3/8 in or less.
  • A squeak. Pad will not fix it. A squeak is a joist problem, and the Floor Joist Calculator settles the span first.

Two floors of tear-out produce about 1 lb of pad per sq ft. A dumpster for 2,400 sq ft of carpet and pad runs $450 to $700, and it lands as one line.

The pad gap is $420. The carpet grade gap is $1,764, and the install rate adds $1,440. Shopping the job on pad price moves 8% of the total.

Pad still decides how the floor feels and whether the warranty survives year six. It is not the line to negotiate first. On the same 1,200 sq ft, engineered hardwood at $7 to $10 installed lands near Bid B, and the Hardwood Calculator prices that swap.

What to order

Buy 8 lb rebond at 7/16 in, $5.40 to $8.10 a yard, for living rooms, stairs, and any carpet carrying a 10-year texture warranty. It costs $420 more than 6 lb on 1,200 sq ft and keeps a floor reading new at year eight.

Buy 6 lb rebond at 3/8 in for bedrooms, rentals and any room due for replacement inside seven years. Frothed foam belongs in a unit you are turning over.

Skip the half-inch memory foam unless the carpet is a plush over 40 oz. Under a 30 oz texture the extra $400 never shows underfoot, and it eats door clearance.

The trade-off is height. A 7/16 in pad plus a half-inch carpet takes 7/8 in at every threshold, so two doors get trimmed at $25 to $50 and the tile needs a reducer. Every estimator sits on the flooring category page.

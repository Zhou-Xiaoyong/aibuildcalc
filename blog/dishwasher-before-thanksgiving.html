Dishwasher Before Thanksgiving? 24 Inches, 12 Gallons, $1,650

Published November 2026 · 7 min read · By AIBuildCalc

A dishwasher needs a 24-inch opening, a dedicated 120-volt circuit, and a 1-1/2-inch drain, and a full rough-in runs $1,650 in most markets this month. Four Thanksgiving loads move 12.4 gallons of water against roughly 40 for the same dishes by hand. Ten days is enough time for the rough-in. It is not enough time for a special order.

Quick answer: measure the slot before you shop. If a machine has been in that space before, the circuit and the drain are already there, and a $549 in-stock unit plus three hours of work finishes the job this weekend. If the space is still a base cabinet, you are buying a circuit and a drain, which is $1,400 to $2,100 and four to six working days. Ten days out, the circuit is the only item that can still sink the schedule, so call the electrician before you pay for the machine. Size the drain first with the Drain Pipe Calculator, because a sink and a dishwasher on one small arm is where most of these inspections get written up.

Twenty-four inches, measured at the cabinet

Every number on a dishwasher spec sheet assumes a rough opening, not a countertop. The opening is 24 inches wide, 24 deep, and 34 high, and the height is what surprises people. A base cabinet runs 34-1/2 inches to the top of the box, and a 1-1/2-inch top brings the surface to 36. That leaves 34-1/2 inches for a machine that stands 33.9 tall on legs with half an inch of adjustment.

Width is where a tight kitchen bites. A 24-inch cabinet has about 22-1/2 inches of clear width between its side panels, so you cannot pull the door off and slide a machine in. The box comes out, or it gets cut out, and a filler strip closes the gap to the next cabinet.

Depth is the quiet constraint. The frame is 22.6 inches deep, and the supply line, the drain hose, and the cord live in the last inch and a half behind it, which is why the opening is 24. A cabinet nailed tight to the drywall with no service gap means notching the drywall or setting the machine 3/4 inch proud, and a proud machine will not fit under a countertop that overhangs an inch.

Then the door swing. Most doors need 27 inches of clear floor when they drop. In a galley kitchen that is fine. In front of an island set 36 inches away, the door lands on a drawer pull. Price the cabinet work with the Cabinet Calculator if a box is coming out.

Twelve gallons instead of forty

Federal law has capped kitchen faucets at 2.2 gallons a minute since 1994, so a double-bowl sink filled to a working level holds about 10 gallons. Thanksgiving generates four of those: the potato pot and the colander, the roasting pan and the carving board, the plates, and the glassware. Forty gallons, and about forty-eight minutes of standing at the sink with your hands in it.

A standard dishwasher under the Energy Star program uses 3.1 gallons a cycle. Four cycles is 12.4 gallons, and the hands-on time is closer to twelve minutes of loading and unloading. That is the honest case for the machine: two hours of your evening, not a water bill. The 27.6 gallons saved is worth about 33 cents. Twenty heavy loads a year saves under $10, which will not pay for the drain fitting, let alone the unit.

The hot water math matters more. Four cycles spread across an evening draw 12.4 gallons at 120 to 140 degrees, which a 50-gallon tank absorbs without a recovery gap, since a gas heater that size rebuilds roughly 35 to 40 gallons an hour. Draw them back to back while three people shower and the tank becomes the limit, not the machine. The 50-gallon water heater numbers cover that failure mode before you add a second hot-water appliance to a house that already runs out.

Path two hides the surprise. A kitchen built after 1990 often has a dishwasher circuit and a drain stub because the builder roughed in for an upgrade option, and the cabinet in that slot was never cut. Those jobs finish in a day. A kitchen from 1965 has neither, and the drain has to tie into the stack, which may sit on the far wall behind the refrigerator.

Path three is where the estimate turns into a range. A dedicated circuit from a panel with two open slots costs $420 in most metros. No open slots adds $500 to $900 for tandems or a subpanel, and a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel from the 1970s becomes a replacement that no electrician will schedule in ten days.

The circuit stops a Thursday install

A dishwasher draws 10 to 12 amps while the heating element is on, which is why manufacturers ask for a dedicated 15-amp circuit and most installs land on a 20-amp with 12-gauge wire. It cannot share one of the two 20-amp small-appliance circuits that feed the countertop, and it cannot share with the disposal unless the circuit was sized for both.

Ground-fault protection is now part of the answer. The 2020 National Electrical Code requires GFCI for kitchen receptacles under 210.8(A)(6), and the 2023 edition names dishwashers in 210.8(D). A GFCI breaker costs $45 more than a plain one and ends the argument at inspection. Check the load in the Amp Calculator before you decide the existing 15-amp will do, and the Thanksgiving load breakdown shows what the rest of the kitchen pulls at the same hour.

Fishing 12-gauge is a two-hour job on an open basement ceiling and five hours through plaster. That labor, not the wire, is what moves a $420 circuit to $700.

One and a half inches of drain, and where the loop goes

The drain is the part that gets improvised. A 7/8-inch corrugated hose ties into the sink's tailpiece through a barbed wye, or into its own trap arm when the sink sits across the room. In the fixture-unit table a domestic dishwasher counts 2 and a kitchen sink counts 2, and a 1-1/2-inch horizontal branch is capped at 3, which is why a plumber runs 2-inch to the wye when both share it.

Then the loop. The hose has to rise to the underside of the countertop before it turns down, or dirty water from a full sink siphons back into the machine. Manufacturers call for that loop at 20 inches above the floor at minimum. Some states that adopt the Uniform Plumbing Code want a deck-mounted air gap instead, which means a chrome fitting on the counter and a second hose down to the disposer. The kit is $25 to $60 and takes twenty minutes. Ask your inspector which one applies before you drill.

The supply side is simpler: a 3/8-inch compression stop on a hot line, 120 degrees incoming, and the internal boost heater takes it to 140 or 150 for sanitize. A cold-only tap will run and will not clean a roasting pan.

One note that costs people a day: run a full cycle with no dishes before the first real load. A weeping supply fitting shows up in ten minutes on a test cycle and in ten hours under a cabinet full of plates, by which point the leak has found the subfloor.

Black Friday falls on the 27th, and dishwasher pricing does drop that week. A $120 discount on a machine that arrives December 4 is worth nothing if the point was the 26th. Buy in stock now, or take the sale knowing the job is a January job.

Thanksgiving dishwasher questions

Can a dishwasher go where a base cabinet is now?

Yes, and it is the most common version of this project. The box comes out with a reciprocating saw, a filler strip closes the gap, and the machine needs a circuit and a drain the cabinet never had. Budget a day plus the electrician's time, and keep the removed cabinet. A 24-inch base box is $180 to $300 new and someone on the block will take it.

Does a dishwasher really need its own circuit?

Manufacturers specify one, and the code path supports it because the heating element pulls 10 to 12 amps on its own. A shared 20-amp circuit with a disposal works only when the circuit was sized for both, which was common in the 1990s. A shared 15-amp with countertop receptacles will nuisance-trip the breaker the first time someone runs the machine and the toaster at once.

Air gap or high loop?

High loop if your inspector accepts it, air gap if the state adopted the Uniform Plumbing Code or the manual names one. The loop is free and invisible. The air gap puts a chrome cap on the sink deck and spills a little water back into the basin when the hose backs up. Neither one fixes a hose hung with a sag, and a sag causes the smell people blame on the machine.

The call

Shine a light into the slot and answer one question: is there a hole there, or is there a cabinet? If the hookups exist, buy a 24-inch in-stock unit this weekend, spend the extra $45 on a GFCI breaker, and run a test cycle before the guests land. That is $549 and one Saturday, and it buys back two hours of every holiday after this one.

If the slot is a cabinet, skip it this month. The rough-in costs the same in February, the electrician has time then, and $1,650 spent in a hurry on a ten-day deadline is how people end up with a drain that sags behind the machine and a cabinet floor that swells by spring. Buy a $60 bus tub and a dish rack, put the money in a January envelope, and let the 26th be about the food.
